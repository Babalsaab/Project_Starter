### 1.1 Marketing Strategy & Planning

1. Start every strategy with clearly defined, measurable OKRs tied to business outcomes (revenue, activation, retention) — not vanity metrics.
2. Segment your total addressable market (TAM) into serviceable obtainable market (SOM) tiers and prioritize the highest-leverage segments first.
3. Perform a SWOT analysis at least quarterly, refreshing competitive intelligence and internal capability assessments.
4. Align the marketing strategy doc with the product roadmap on a shared quarterly cadence so launches never happen in a vacuum.
5. Build a single-page strategic brief (audience, positioning, channels, KPIs, budget) that every team member can reference — avoid 50-slide decks no one reads.
6. Define the marketing funnel stages explicitly (awareness → consideration → conversion → retention → advocacy) and assign owners to each.
7. Use a rolling 90-day plan with weekly check-ins rather than rigid annual plans that become stale.
8. Allocate at least 10-20% of budget to experimentation on unproven channels or tactics each quarter.
9. Map every initiative to a customer journey stage; reject any tactic that cannot be tied to a specific stage.
10. Maintain a competitive war room — a living document tracking competitor positioning, pricing, messaging, and feature launches.
11. Create a RACI matrix for every campaign: who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed.
12. Set leading indicators (e.g., MQL velocity, pipeline coverage ratio) alongside lagging indicators (e.g., revenue, churn).
13. Conduct a portfolio review of all active campaigns monthly; kill the bottom 20% and reallocate budget upward.
14. Build scenario-planning models (bull / base / bear) for budget allocation so the team can pivot quickly if conditions change.
15. Document assumptions behind every forecast explicitly — hidden assumptions are the #1 cause of missed targets.
16. Create a "marketing calendar of record" in a shared tool (Asana, Monday, Notion) that includes product launches, seasonal events, and external industry dates.
17. Ensure messaging consistency by maintaining an approved messaging matrix (audience × pain point × value prop × proof point).
18. Use the "Jobs to Be Done" framework to identify why customers hire your product, not just who they are demographically.
19. Define a clear ICP (Ideal Customer Profile) with firmographic, technographic, and behavioral attributes.
20. Establish a feedback loop between marketing and sales/CS — weekly pipeline review + monthly win/loss analysis.
21. Prioritize channels by expected CAC payback period, not just top-of-funnel volume.
22. Run a "pre-mortem" before major launches: imagine the campaign failed and list every reason why.
23. Build brand guidelines that cover tone, voice, visual identity, and messaging guardrails in a single accessible document.
24. Track share of voice (SOV) relative to competitors across key channels (search, social, press).
25. Use a weighted scoring model to prioritize marketing initiatives (impact × confidence × ease).
26. Build dashboards with a hierarchy: executive summary → channel performance → campaign detail → experiment results.
27. Document every test hypothesis, result, and learning in a shared "test & learn" log.
28. Maintain a "marketing tech stack map" showing every tool, its owner, cost, and integration points.
29. Conduct annual or semi-annual brand health tracking studies (aided/unaided awareness, NPS, brand attributes).
30. Align incentive structures so marketing is rewarded on pipeline and revenue contribution, not just lead volume.
31. Use cohort-based analysis rather than aggregate metrics to understand true marketing impact over time.
32. Build a customer lifecycle revenue model that accounts for acquisition cost, activation rate, expansion revenue, and churn.
33. Create a "marketing playbook" for repeatable motions (product launch, seasonal campaign, partner co-marketing).
34. Assign a "marketing DRI" (Directly Responsible Individual) to every initiative — shared ownership is no ownership.
35. Establish clear SLAs between marketing and sales (e.g., lead follow-up within 4 hours, lead scoring threshold for MQL → SQL handoff).
36. Benchmark performance against industry standards but optimize against your own historical baselines.
37. Use attribution modeling (multi-touch, not just last-click) to understand true channel contribution.
38. Plan for seasonality explicitly — build a historical seasonality index and adjust targets monthly.
39. Integrate customer advisory board (CAB) feedback directly into strategic planning.
40. Maintain a risk register for marketing: regulatory changes, platform algorithm shifts, key vendor dependencies.
41. Ensure every dollar of spend has a tracking mechanism — UTM parameters, promo codes, unique landing pages.
42. Run a "marketing audit" every 6 months: review all assets, messaging, and channels for staleness or inconsistency.
43. Build a content-channel matrix showing which content types perform best on which channels.
44. Invest in marketing operations (MOps) early — clean data and automated workflows compound value over time.
45. Use a "minimum viable campaign" approach for new initiatives: launch lean, measure fast, scale what works.
46. Create audience personas that include behavioral triggers and objections, not just demographics.
47. Establish a brand voice chart with "we are / we are not" columns (e.g., "confident, not arrogant").
48. Design campaigns for the full buying committee in B2B: champion, decision-maker, influencer, blocker.
49. Track marketing efficiency ratio (MER) = total revenue / total marketing spend as a north-star health metric.
50. Conduct "message testing" with real prospects before committing to campaign messaging at scale.
51. Always have a "control" version running in every channel so you can measure incremental lift.
52. Build a marketing data dictionary so everyone uses the same definitions for MQL, SQL, opportunity, etc.

### 1.2 Product Marketing

1. Own the positioning: write a positioning statement (For [target], [product] is the [category] that [key benefit] because [reason to believe]) and pressure-test it with customers.
2. Conduct competitive battlecards for the sales team — update them monthly with fresh intel.
3. Create a launch tier system (T1 = major, T2 = medium, T3 = minor) with defined playbooks for each tier.
4. Write the press release and FAQ before the product is built (Amazon's "working backwards" method).
5. Develop a messaging hierarchy: headline → sub-headline → 3 pillars → proof points → objection handlers.
6. Build a "one-pager" for every product/feature that any team member can use to explain value in 60 seconds.
7. Run customer interviews (minimum 10-15) before every major positioning or messaging change.
8. Create use-case-specific landing pages rather than a single generic product page.
9. Develop sales enablement materials: pitch decks, demo scripts, ROI calculators, case studies.
10. Own the "narrative arc" — craft a compelling story that connects market trends → customer problems → your solution → outcomes.
11. Map features to benefits to outcomes: customers don't buy features, they buy outcomes.
12. Build a competitive matrix comparing your product on the dimensions that matter most to buyers.
13. Run "message mining" on G2 reviews, support tickets, and sales call recordings to find authentic customer language.
14. Create persona-specific talk tracks for sales — what resonates with a CFO is different from a developer.
15. Define and own the product category; if no category exists, create and name it.
16. Measure product-market fit quantitatively: use Sean Ellis's "very disappointed" survey (target > 40%).
17. Develop customer case studies with the STAR format: Situation, Task, Action, Result (with quantified outcomes).
18. Create "day in the life" narratives showing before/after your product for each persona.
19. Build an analyst relations program: brief Gartner, Forrester, and niche analysts proactively.
20. Own the onboarding messaging and in-app copy for the first 7 days — product marketing, not just product.
21. Create "migration guides" and "switching cost calculators" to reduce barriers for competitive wins.
22. Track win/loss reasons systematically — tag every closed-won and closed-lost deal with primary reason.
23. Develop pricing page messaging that anchors value before revealing price.
24. Run beta programs with structured feedback loops — NPS survey + qualitative interview at beta close.
25. Build a "content engine" around each product launch: blog post, social thread, email, webinar, case study.
26. Create internal training programs so CS and sales can articulate new features confidently within 24 hours of launch.
27. Use "proof point libraries" — curated stats, quotes, and results that any team member can pull from.
28. Conduct quarterly positioning audits: does the market still perceive us the way we intend?
29. Build "objection playbooks" for the top 10 objections sales encounters.
30. Create comparison pages for top competitors — own the narrative before third-party review sites do.
31. Develop "feature announcement" templates for consistent, high-quality release communications.
32. Use customer advisory boards to validate messaging before broad launch.
33. Measure time-to-first-value (TTFV) for new users and work with product to reduce it.
34. Write crisp, jargon-free value propositions — if your grandmother can't understand it, rewrite it.
35. Build a customer reference program with tiered engagement (logo use, quote, case study, speaking).
36. Own the product narrative on third-party review sites (G2, Capterra, TrustRadius).
37. Create "cheat sheets" and "quick start guides" that complement product documentation.
38. Run pricing research (Van Westendorp, Gabor-Granger, or conjoint analysis) before major pricing changes.
39. Build "solution briefs" for vertical/industry-specific positioning.
40. Track competitive mention velocity — how often competitors appear in sales conversations.
41. Create demo environments and sandbox accounts that prospects can explore self-serve.
42. Develop a "launch retrospective" template and run it after every T1 and T2 launch.
43. Build segment-specific ROI models that prospects can customize with their own numbers.
44. Own the relationship between marketing and product management — be the "voice of the market" in product decisions.
45. Create a "competitive intelligence" Slack channel or newsletter for real-time intel sharing.
46. Develop "packaging & bundling" recommendations based on customer usage patterns.
47. Write executive briefing documents for enterprise sales conversations.
48. Build a "product marketing impact dashboard" tracking launch metrics, win rates, and content usage.
49. Use "message testing" tools (Wynter, UserTesting) to validate messaging with your ICP before launch.
50. Document the "why now" for every product — articulate the market urgency that makes this the right time.

### 1.3 Brand Strategy & Positioning

1. Define brand positioning with a clear differentiation statement that articulates what you do differently and why it matters.
2. Create a brand architecture document if you have multiple products/sub-brands — define relationships (branded house vs. house of brands).
3. Develop a brand pyramid: purpose → values → personality → tone of voice → visual identity.
4. Conduct brand perception research annually — measure aided awareness, unaided awareness, and key attribute associations.
5. Build a comprehensive brand book covering logo usage, color palette (with hex/RGB/CMYK), typography, imagery style, and iconography.
6. Define brand voice with a personality spectrum (e.g., "professional but not stuffy, witty but not silly").
7. Create a "brand filter" — a set of questions every piece of content must pass before publication.
8. Establish brand naming conventions for products, features, and tiers with a documented naming framework.
9. Monitor brand sentiment across social media, reviews, and press using automated tools (Brandwatch, Mention).
10. Build a brand narrative that connects your founding story to the customer's aspirations.
11. Conduct competitive brand audits — map competitor positioning on a 2×2 matrix to find whitespace.
12. Develop a sonic identity and motion design language for video/audio touchpoints.
13. Create brand templates for every channel (social, email, presentation, print) to ensure consistency at scale.
14. Define "brand tension" — the dynamic quality that makes the brand interesting (e.g., "accessible expertise").
15. Build a brand asset library (DAM) with approved logos, images, icons, and templates accessible to all teams.
16. Establish brand governance: who can approve new brand expressions and what requires escalation.
17. Create a "brand DO and DON'T" guide with visual examples of correct and incorrect usage.
18. Develop co-branding guidelines for partnerships — define logo placement, sizing, and clearance rules.
19. Run a brand equity valuation exercise periodically to quantify the financial value of the brand.
20. Build emotional brand attributes — define how customers should feel when they interact with your brand.
21. Create a competitive positioning map updated quarterly showing all major players on key differentiating dimensions.
22. Develop a crisis communication playbook that protects brand reputation during incidents.
23. Track Net Promoter Score (NPS) and correlate it with brand perception metrics.
24. Build a brand storytelling framework: hero (customer) → challenge → guide (your brand) → plan → success.
25. Conduct "brand immersion" sessions for new hires so everyone can articulate the brand consistently.
26. Define brand extension criteria: under what conditions would you extend the brand into new categories?
27. Create channel-specific brand expression guidelines (LinkedIn ≠ TikTok ≠ email ≠ app).
28. Build a "brand health scorecard" with quarterly tracking of awareness, consideration, preference, and loyalty.
29. Develop a tagline testing methodology — test 5-10 options with real customers before committing.
30. Establish a "brand council" with cross-functional representatives to ensure brand consistency.
31. Create a visual identity evolution plan — how will the brand mature over the next 3-5 years?
32. Define brand archetypes and use them to guide creative direction (e.g., "the Sage," "the Creator").
33. Build a language style guide with approved terminology, capitalization rules, and writing conventions.
34. Conduct "touchpoint audits" — review every customer interaction point for brand consistency.
35. Develop a social responsibility / ESG narrative that authentically connects to the brand purpose.
36. Create an internal brand engagement program — employees are the most credible brand ambassadors.
37. Build a rebranding decision framework: when to refresh, when to evolve, when to rebrand completely.
38. Track share of search (branded search volume) as a proxy for brand awareness.
39. Develop a "brand experience" map showing how brand is expressed across the full customer journey.
40. Create a trademark and IP protection strategy — register marks in all relevant markets.
41. Build a "brand perception gap analysis" comparing intended positioning with actual customer perception.
42. Define the brand's relationship to culture — how does it show up in cultural conversations authentically?
43. Create guidelines for user-generated content (UGC) — what's encouraged, what needs approval.
44. Develop a thought leadership strategy that positions executives as category authorities.
45. Build a "brand lexicon" — specific words and phrases the brand always uses and always avoids.
46. Conduct "brand switching" research to understand why customers leave and join.
47. Create "brand experience principles" — 3-5 guiding principles for how every interaction should feel.
48. Develop a brand measurement model that connects brand metrics to business outcomes.
49. Build a competitive messaging tracker — monitor how competitor positioning changes over time.
50. Establish a "brand debt" tracker — catalog all off-brand materials that need updating.

### 1.4 Growth Marketing

1. Build a growth model (spreadsheet or tool) that maps every lever: traffic × conversion × activation × retention × referral × revenue.
2. Define a "North Star Metric" that captures the core value delivered to customers (e.g., weekly active users, messages sent, revenue per user).
3. Run a minimum of 3-5 experiments per week across channels and funnel stages — velocity of learning matters more than any single test.
4. Use the ICE framework (Impact × Confidence × Ease) to prioritize experiments.
5. Build a growth "backlog" in a kanban board: ideas → prioritized → in-test → analyzing → scaled / killed.
6. Instrument every touchpoint with event tracking (Segment, Amplitude, Mixpanel) before running experiments.
7. Define statistical significance thresholds before running tests — don't peek at results early.
8. Map the "aha moment" (the action correlated with retention) and build activation flows that drive users to it.
9. Analyze user cohorts by acquisition source, signup date, and behavior to identify retention drivers.
10. Build a referral/viral loop into the product — design sharing mechanics that are native to the user experience.
11. Optimize the signup flow relentlessly: reduce fields, add social auth, show social proof, remove friction.
12. Use onboarding checklists and progress bars to guide new users to activation milestones.
13. Implement behavioral email/push sequences triggered by user actions (or inaction) — not just time-based drips.
14. Build paywall experiments: test pricing, trial length, feature gating, and upgrade triggers.
15. Create a "growth loop" diagram showing how new users → actions → outputs → new users (virality, content, paid).
16. Analyze drop-off at every funnel step weekly — the biggest leak is your biggest opportunity.
17. Run "fake door" tests to validate demand before building features.
18. Implement progressive profiling — gather user data gradually rather than in a long signup form.
19. Build a reactivation playbook for churned or dormant users — win-back campaigns with specific incentives.
20. Use deep linking so every marketing touchpoint drops users into the right in-app screen.
21. Create landing page templates optimized for each traffic source (search, social, email, referral).
22. A/B test CTAs, headlines, hero images, and social proof independently — don't bundle changes.
23. Build a retention curve for each cohort and identify the "flattening point" — that's your retention benchmark.
24. Use engagement scoring to identify users at risk of churn and trigger intervention campaigns.
25. Implement server-side experimentation for critical flows to avoid flicker and ensure clean measurement.
26. Run pricing experiments with care: use geographic segmentation or cohort-based tests to minimize cannibalization.
27. Build a "growth accounting" framework: new users + resurrected users − churned users = net growth.
28. Measure "time to value" (TTV) — how long from signup to first meaningful outcome — and relentlessly reduce it.
29. Create a "habit loop" in the product: trigger → action → variable reward → investment.
30. Use push notification and email frequency capping to avoid notification fatigue.
31. Build a "power user" profile: what do your best users have in common? Optimize acquisition to find more of them.
32. Implement cross-sell and upsell triggers based on usage patterns and feature engagement.
33. Run qualitative research (user interviews, session recordings) alongside quantitative experiments.
34. Build a self-serve onboarding flow that eliminates the need for human-assisted setup.
35. Create "magic number" analysis: identify the specific action count (e.g., "users who add 3 friends in week 1") that predicts retention.
36. Use holdout groups for every major initiative to measure true incremental impact.
37. Implement feature flags for gradual rollouts and quick rollbacks.
38. Build a growth newsletter or "weekly experiment digest" to share learnings across the team.
39. Create a "growth scorecard" reviewed weekly: experiments run, win rate, cumulative impact.
40. Use "Minimum Detectable Effect" (MDE) calculations to properly size experiments before launch.
41. Build lifecycle stages with clear graduation criteria: new → activated → engaged → power user → advocate.
42. Implement product-led growth (PLG) mechanics: free tier, self-serve upgrade, in-app expansion.
43. Track expansion revenue (upsell + cross-sell) as a percentage of total revenue — healthy SaaS targets >30%.
44. Run "negative experiments" — deliberately test removing features or flows to see if they're actually needed.
45. Build a "growth model sensitivity analysis" — which input variable has the biggest impact on output?
46. Create urgency and scarcity ethically: limited-time offers, usage-based triggers, social proof.
47. Implement attribution tracking that connects acquisition source to lifetime value, not just first conversion.
48. Build dashboards that show leading metrics (daily active users, activation rate) alongside lagging metrics (revenue, LTV).
49. Run "teardown" analyses of competitor onboarding and growth mechanics quarterly.
50. Create a "growth playbook" documenting every winning experiment with implementation details so future team members can replicate.

### 1.5 Customer Research & Insights (Marketing Side)

1. Conduct continuous discovery interviews — aim for at least 5 customer conversations per week across the marketing team.
2. Build a research repository (Dovetail, Notion, Airtable) where all insights are tagged, searchable, and accessible.
3. Triangulate insights: combine qualitative (interviews, usability tests) with quantitative (surveys, analytics, A/B tests).
4. Define research questions before choosing methodology — don't default to surveys for everything.
5. Use "Jobs to Be Done" interview techniques to uncover the causal mechanisms behind purchase decisions.
6. Conduct "message mining" — analyze customer support tickets, reviews, and social mentions for authentic language.
7. Build customer journey maps based on research, not assumptions — interview customers at each stage.
8. Run competitive win/loss interviews: talk to customers who chose you AND who chose a competitor.
9. Use the "5 Whys" technique in interviews to get past surface-level answers.
10. Create customer personas grounded in behavioral data and validated with qualitative research — not demographic fiction.
11. Segment survey data by behavior (power users vs. casual) and acquisition channel, not just demographics.
12. Run quarterly NPS surveys with mandatory open-text follow-up questions to capture qualitative context.
13. Build a "voice of customer" program that systematically routes feedback to product and marketing teams.
14. Conduct "contextual inquiry" — observe customers using your product in their natural environment.
15. Use card sorting and tree testing for information architecture decisions.
16. Run preference tests and A/B polls for creative and messaging decisions before committing to production.
17. Build a research panel of customers who have opted in to participate in studies — maintain it actively.
18. Conduct annual or semi-annual segmentation studies using cluster analysis on behavioral and attitudinal data.
19. Use "diary studies" for understanding longitudinal behavior patterns over days or weeks.
20. Analyze search query data (both internal site search and Google Search Console) for insight into customer intent.
21. Build "empathy maps" for each persona: what they think, feel, say, and do.
22. Run usability tests on landing pages and marketing funnels, not just product features.
23. Conduct pricing research (Van Westendorp, MaxDiff, conjoint) before major pricing decisions.
24. Use social listening tools to monitor brand mentions, sentiment trends, and emerging topics.
25. Build a "research impact tracker" — document how insights influenced decisions and outcomes.
26. Run "concept tests" for new product ideas or marketing campaigns before full investment.
27. Conduct "first-click" tests on landing pages to see where users expect to click first.
28. Use heatmaps and session recordings (Hotjar, FullStory) to complement quantitative funnel data.
29. Run "unmoderated" remote tests for scale and "moderated" tests for depth — use both.
30. Build a "customer insight of the week" communication to keep insights top-of-mind across the org.
31. Conduct market sizing research using both top-down (TAM/SAM/SOM) and bottom-up approaches.
32. Use MaxDiff analysis to prioritize feature importance or messaging preference with statistical rigor.
33. Run "intercept surveys" at key moments in the customer journey (post-purchase, post-onboarding, pre-churn).
34. Build a competitive intelligence database updated monthly with pricing, features, messaging, and reviews.
35. Conduct brand tracking studies that measure awareness, consideration, and preference over time.
36. Use ethnographic methods for deeply understanding customer contexts and pain points.
37. Run "fake door" tests as a form of demand research — measure click-through on proposed features before building.
38. Analyze customer support ticket themes monthly for emerging pain points and messaging opportunities.
39. Conduct "jobs atlas" mapping — visualize all the jobs customers are trying to do and where your product fits.
40. Build a research ethics framework: informed consent, data privacy, appropriate incentives, no deception.
41. Use "think-aloud" protocols in usability testing to capture real-time reasoning.
42. Run satisfaction surveys (CSAT) at specific touchpoints, not just overall relationship surveys.
43. Conduct "switchback" research: interview customers who left and came back to understand re-engagement triggers.
44. Build a "data wall" (physical or digital) where the team can see key insights and customer quotes daily.
45. Use A/B testing of survey questions to improve response rates and data quality.
46. Run "willingness to pay" research before launching premium features or new pricing tiers.
47. Conduct benchmarking studies comparing your UX metrics to industry standards.
48. Use "prototype testing" with clickable mockups to validate concepts before engineering investment.
49. Build a "research roadmap" aligned to the marketing and product roadmap — anticipate research needs 1-2 quarters ahead.
50. Create "insight snapshots" — one-page summaries of key findings with clear implications and recommended actions.

### 2.1 Performance Marketing (Paid Acquisition)

1. Set clear CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost) targets by channel, segmented by customer tier and LTV.
2. Structure ad accounts hierarchically: campaign (objective) → ad set (audience) → ad (creative) — keep it organized.
3. Implement proper conversion tracking with server-side events (CAPI for Meta, enhanced conversions for Google) to survive cookie deprecation.
4. Build a creative testing framework: test one variable at a time (headline, image, CTA, offer) with sufficient budget per variant.
5. Use broad targeting with smart bidding algorithms — over-segmenting audiences limits algorithmic learning.
6. Launch campaigns with "learning phase" budgets (50 conversions per week per ad set on Meta) to exit learning phase quickly.
7. Build a creative production pipeline: brief → concept → design → copy → review → launch — with SLAs at each step.
8. Refresh creative every 2-4 weeks to combat ad fatigue — monitor frequency and CTR decay.
9. Use UTM parameters consistently: source, medium, campaign, content, term — document conventions in a UTM guide.
10. Implement post-click landing page optimization: match ad message → landing page headline → CTA for "scent trail" consistency.
11. Build lookalike/similar audiences from your highest-value customers (top 1-5% LTV), not just all converters.
12. Use exclusion audiences to avoid wasting spend on existing customers and recent converters.
13. Run incrementality tests (holdout studies, geo-lift tests) to measure true lift beyond platform-reported conversions.
14. Implement dayparting only after collecting 2-4 weeks of hourly conversion data — don't assume patterns.
15. Build a "creative library" organized by format, audience, funnel stage, and performance tier.
16. Use dynamic creative optimization (DCO) to test element combinations at scale.
17. Allocate budget using a 70/20/10 framework: 70% proven tactics, 20% scaling promising tests, 10% experimental.
18. Set up automated rules for budget pacing and anomaly alerts (cost spikes, CTR drops, conversion droughts).
19. Run retargeting campaigns with frequency caps and fresh creative — stale retargeting ads destroy brand perception.
20. Build a "performance marketing dashboard" with daily, weekly, and monthly cadences showing spend, CAC, ROAS, and LTV.
21. Use offline conversion imports to feed downstream conversion data (sign-up → activation → payment) back to ad platforms.
22. Implement value-based bidding — bid to value (LTV), not just conversion count.
23. Test video creative alongside static — video often drives lower CPMs and higher engagement on social platforms.
24. Build separate campaigns for prospecting vs. retargeting vs. retention to control budgets independently.
25. Use "creative scoring" to systematically rate ad performance on engagement, conversion, and efficiency.
26. Implement proper attribution windows by platform (Meta: 7-day click, 1-day view; Google: 30-day click).
27. Run platform-specific A/B tests using built-in tools (Meta's A/B test, Google Experiments) for statistical rigor.
28. Create "ad fatigue alerts" when frequency exceeds 3-4x per week for prospecting audiences.
29. Build a competitive ad intelligence practice — use Meta Ad Library and tools like Pathmatics to monitor competitor creative.
30. Use negative keywords (search) and placement exclusions (display/video) aggressively to reduce wasted spend.
31. Implement cross-channel frequency capping to prevent bombarding users across platforms.
32. Test different bidding strategies (maximize conversions, target CPA, target ROAS) with controlled experiments.
33. Build a "media mix model" (MMM) annually to understand cross-channel interactions and optimal allocation.
34. Use responsive search ads (RSAs) with at least 10 unique headlines and 4 descriptions in Google Ads.
35. Create platform-specific creative: what works on Meta doesn't work on Google Display doesn't work on TikTok.
36. Implement "campaign experiments" in Google Ads to test bidding changes without risking the entire campaign.
37. Build an automated reporting pipeline that pulls from all ad platform APIs into a single dashboard.
38. Run brand lift studies on Meta and YouTube to measure awareness and consideration impact of paid campaigns.
39. Use audience signals (not restrictions) in Performance Max to guide the algorithm without limiting it.
40. Implement proper Floodlight or conversion linker tags for full cross-device conversion tracking.
41. Run "creative velocity" analysis: track how many new creatives you launch per week and correlate with performance.
42. Use first-party data (CRM lists, email subscribers) for custom audiences and seed audiences for lookalikes.
43. Build a "paid media playbook" for each campaign type (awareness, consideration, conversion, retention).
44. Implement "budget fluidity" across channels — move spend toward the best-performing channel weekly.
45. Test different landing page variants per audience segment — personalize the post-click experience.
46. Use impression share and top impression rate as competitive metrics in Google Search campaigns.
47. Run "creative concept tests" with small budgets ($500-1000) before scaling winning concepts.
48. Build a "campaign naming convention" document for consistent ad account hygiene across the team.
49. Use automated scripts (Google Ads scripts, Meta Rules) for routine optimizations and anomaly detection.
50. Track and optimize the entire funnel from impression → click → landing page → signup → activation → payment — not just platform metrics.

### 2.2 Search Engine Marketing (SEM / PPC)

1. Build keyword lists using search intent tiers: navigational → informational → commercial → transactional — bid highest on transactional.
2. Use Single Keyword Ad Groups (SKAGs) or tightly themed ad groups for high-value terms to maximize relevance.
3. Implement comprehensive negative keyword lists organized by theme (competitors, free, jobs, unrelated terms).
4. Write ad copy that includes the primary keyword in the headline, speaks to the searcher's intent, and has a clear CTA.
5. Use all available ad extensions: sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets, price extensions, image extensions.
6. Build separate campaigns for brand vs. non-brand terms to control budgets and measure performance independently.
7. Implement a bid strategy that aligns with your goals: tCPA for volume, tROAS for efficiency, maximize conversions for learning.
8. Use "Search Terms Report" weekly to find new negative keywords and new keyword opportunities.
9. Build keyword-to-landing-page mapping to ensure every high-value keyword has a dedicated, relevant landing page.
10. Implement dynamic keyword insertion (DKI) carefully — only where the inserted term reads naturally.
11. Use RSAs (Responsive Search Ads) with diverse headlines: include keywords, benefits, CTAs, social proof, and urgency.
12. Pin critical headlines (brand name, primary value prop) but leave most positions unpinned for algorithmic optimization.
13. Build campaign structures that separate by match type if using manual bidding — allow smart bidding to manage match types when using automated strategies.
14. Monitor Quality Score components: expected CTR, ad relevance, landing page experience — improve the lowest one first.
15. Use auction insights to monitor competitive activity: impression share, overlap rate, outranking share.
16. Implement ad scheduling based on conversion rate by hour and day — pause or reduce bids during low-converting periods.
17. Build a "search query taxonomy" — categorize all search queries by intent, topic, and funnel stage.
18. Use Performance Max campaigns for broad coverage but maintain standard search campaigns for core high-value terms.
19. Implement location targeting and bid adjustments based on geographic performance data.
20. Build remarketing lists for search ads (RLSA) — bid higher on return visitors who already know your brand.
21. Use "Observation" audience targeting to gather data without restricting reach, then move to "Targeting" when confident.
22. Create competitor campaigns (bidding on competitor brand names) with clear differentiation messaging.
23. Implement conversion tracking at multiple funnel stages: click → lead → qualified lead → opportunity → customer.
24. Use offline conversion tracking to feed CRM outcomes back to Google Ads for smarter bidding.
25. Build a keyword research cadence: monthly for new opportunities, weekly for negatives and search term analysis.

### 2.3 Search Engine Optimization (SEO)

1. Start with keyword research grounded in search intent: categorize every target keyword as informational, navigational, commercial, or transactional.
2. Build a keyword universe spreadsheet with columns for: keyword, monthly volume, difficulty, intent, current rank, target page, priority.
3. Create pillar/cluster content architecture: one comprehensive pillar page per topic cluster, supported by interlinked cluster articles.
4. Optimize title tags using the formula: [Primary Keyword] — [Benefit/Modifier] | [Brand Name] (keep under 60 characters).
5. Write meta descriptions that include the target keyword, a clear value proposition, and a CTA (keep under 155 characters).
6. Use a single H1 tag per page containing the primary keyword; use H2-H4 for supporting keywords and subtopics.
7. Build internal linking strategically: every page should have 3-5 contextual internal links; use descriptive anchor text.
8. Implement technical SEO foundations: clean URL structure, XML sitemap, robots.txt, canonical tags, HTTPS.
9. Ensure Core Web Vitals pass on every key page: LCP < 2.5s, INP < 200ms, CLS < 0.1.
10. Implement structured data (schema.org) for relevant content types: FAQ, HowTo, Product, Review, Article, Organization.
11. Conduct quarterly technical SEO audits using Screaming Frog or Sitebulb: check for broken links, duplicate content, thin pages, crawl errors.
12. Build a backlink strategy focused on quality over quantity: target authoritative domains in your niche.
13. Create "linkable assets" — original research, data studies, interactive tools, templates — that naturally attract backlinks.
14. Monitor and disavow toxic backlinks that could trigger algorithmic penalties.
15. Optimize for featured snippets: use question-based H2s, concise answer paragraphs, lists, and tables.
16. Build content for "People Also Ask" (PAA) opportunities — structure articles with FAQ sections.
17. Implement hreflang tags correctly if targeting multiple languages or regions.
18. Use 301 redirects for permanently moved pages; never use 302 redirects for permanent moves.
19. Build a "content decay" monitoring system — identify pages losing traffic and update them proactively.
20. Optimize images: compress files, use descriptive file names, write alt text with keywords, implement lazy loading.
21. Create a programmatic SEO strategy for scalable content: templates × data = pages (e.g., "/tools/[city]" or "/compare/[product-a]-vs-[product-b]").
22. Build topical authority by covering a subject comprehensively — search engines reward depth and breadth.
23. Monitor search console data weekly: impressions, clicks, CTR, position — for top 50 pages and queries.
24. Implement proper pagination with rel="next" and rel="prev" or infinite scroll with proper indexation.
25. Build an SEO changelog — document every change (title tag update, redirect, content update) with dates and expected impact.
26. Use log file analysis to understand how Googlebot crawls your site — identify crawl budget waste.
27. Optimize for AI Overviews and LLM citations by providing clear, authoritative, structured content.
28. Build content that earns "entity authority" — be the canonical source for key topics in your domain.
29. Use Search Console's "Indexing" report to identify and fix pages that aren't being indexed.
30. Implement breadcrumb navigation with structured data for improved SERP display and internal linking.
31. Conduct content gap analysis vs. top 3 competitors: what topics do they rank for that you don't?
32. Build a "keyword cannibalization" audit — ensure only one page targets each primary keyword.
33. Optimize for local SEO if applicable: Google Business Profile, local citations, location-specific pages.
34. Use "content refresh" as a strategy: update existing high-performing content rather than always creating new.
35. Build a link intersection analysis: find sites that link to 2+ competitors but not to you — those are your best prospects.
36. Implement site speed optimizations: CDN, browser caching, code minification, image compression, lazy loading.
37. Create "hub pages" that serve as comprehensive resource centers for key topics.
38. Monitor algorithm updates (Google Search Central blog, industry trackers) and correlate with traffic changes.
39. Build author authority: author bios, author pages, author schema, credentials for EEAT signals.
40. Use "search intent matching" as the primary ranking factor: if the top 10 results are all listicles, create a listicle.
41. Implement proper mobile optimization: responsive design, touch-friendly elements, no intrusive interstitials.
42. Build a "link reclamation" program — find broken links pointing to your site and fix them.
43. Create "comparison" and "alternative" pages targeting bottom-of-funnel competitor keywords.
44. Use Google Trends and seasonal data to time content publication for maximum impact.
45. Build a technical SEO monitoring stack: automated alerts for indexing drops, crawl errors, speed regressions.
46. Implement content pruning: remove or consolidate thin, outdated, or duplicate content that drags down site quality.
47. Create a "keyword map" document that assigns every key page a primary keyword, secondary keywords, and related terms.
48. Use rendering analysis (Google's URL Inspection tool) to ensure JS-heavy pages are fully indexable.
49. Build a "SERP feature strategy" — target the specific SERP features (knowledge panel, image pack, video carousel) relevant to your keywords.
50. Track organic traffic contribution to pipeline and revenue, not just rankings and traffic — tie SEO to business outcomes.

### 3.2 Marketing Copywriting (Ads, Landing Pages)

1. Lead with the benefit, not the feature — "Save 10 hours a week" beats "AI-powered automation."
2. Write headlines using the 4U framework: Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific, Useful — aim for at least 3 of 4.
3. Use "message-match" — the ad headline and the landing page headline should echo the same promise.
4. Keep landing page copy scannable: short paragraphs (2-3 sentences), subheadings every 3-4 paragraphs, bullet points for features.
5. Write one CTA per landing page — multiple CTAs dilute focus and reduce conversion rates.
6. Use "power words" that trigger emotion: free, instantly, proven, guaranteed, exclusive, limited.
7. Build a "headline swipe file" — collect high-performing headlines from competitors, ads, and direct response copywriting.
8. Test long-form vs. short-form landing pages: high-consideration products often need more copy, not less.
9. Write for the "Stage of Awareness" (Eugene Schwartz): unaware → problem-aware → solution-aware → product-aware → most aware.
10. Use social proof strategically: testimonials, logos, user counts, awards, press mentions, star ratings.
11. Write "above the fold" to answer three questions: What is it? Who is it for? Why should I care?
12. Use specificity to build credibility: "2,847 companies" beats "thousands of companies."
13. Write CTAs with action + outcome: "Start my free trial" beats "Submit" or "Sign up."
14. Build urgency ethically: limited-time offers, countdown timers, usage-based triggers — never fake scarcity.
15. Use "bucket brigade" transitions to keep readers moving: "Here's the thing...", "But wait...", "It gets better..."
16. Write meta-descriptions and ad copy with character limits in mind — front-load the most important info.
17. Use "pattern interrupts" — break expected copy patterns with unexpected formats, visuals, or statements.
18. Write for mobile first: shorter sentences, more white space, tap-friendly CTAs.
19. Build "copy templates" for repeatable ad formats: problem-agitate-solve (PAS), attention-interest-desire-action (AIDA), before-after-bridge (BAB).
20. Use "loss aversion" framing: "Don't lose $X per month" can be more compelling than "Save $X per month."
21. Test emotional vs. rational copy — different audiences and products respond to different motivational drivers.
22. Write alt text and image captions as additional copy real estate — they're read by more people than you think.
23. Use conversational copy that mirrors how customers actually talk — avoid jargon and corporate speak.
24. Build a "landing page copy checklist": headline, sub-headline, hero image, benefits, social proof, CTA, FAQ, objection handling.
25. Write "objection-busting" copy sections that address the top 3-5 concerns directly on the page.
26. Use "storytelling" in long-form copy: customer stories, case studies, and transformation narratives.
27. Test different "value proposition" framings: outcome-based, comparison-based, ROI-based, emotional-based.
28. Write for scanning patterns: F-pattern for text-heavy pages, Z-pattern for visual pages.
29. Use "anchoring" in pricing copy: show the higher price first, then reveal the deal.
30. Build "modular copy blocks" that can be assembled and rearranged for different landing page tests.
31. Write email subject lines that create curiosity, urgency, or personal relevance — test open rates relentlessly.
32. Use "you" more than "we" — make the copy about the customer, not the company.
33. Write "risk reversal" copy: money-back guarantees, free trials, "cancel anytime" promises reduce purchase anxiety.
34. Build a "voice and tone" reference document with examples of on-brand and off-brand copy.
35. Test headline formulas: "How to [achieve X] without [pain Y]", "[Number] ways to [benefit]", "The [adjective] guide to [topic]".
36. Use micro-copy (button text, form labels, error messages, tooltips) as an opportunity to reinforce brand voice.
37. Write "bridge copy" — the transition paragraphs between sections that keep readers flowing down the page.
38. Build a "USP (Unique Selling Proposition)" statement and include it on every landing page.
39. Use "directional cues" — arrows, eye-line of images, and layout flow to guide attention to the CTA.
40. Test pricing presentation: monthly vs. annual, per-user vs. flat rate, with vs. without cents.
41. Write "post-conversion" copy: thank you pages, confirmation emails, and onboarding messages that reinforce the purchase decision.
42. Use "comparison copy" on competitor alternative pages — be factual and specific, not generic and vague.
43. Build a "copy testing" cadence: test one element per two-week period minimum for statistical significance.
44. Write "curiosity gaps" in ad copy — give enough to interest, not enough to satisfy, driving the click.
45. Use "qualifying copy" to filter out non-ideal customers — "Built for teams of 50+" saves everyone time.
46. Build a "headline testing matrix" — test value prop, format, length, and emotional tone systematically.
47. Write "FAQ sections" on landing pages — they handle objections, add keyword-rich content, and build trust.
48. Use "data visualization" in copy — charts, graphs, and visual stats are more persuasive than text alone.
49. Test "long-tail" keyword-specific landing pages vs. broad category pages for SEM traffic.
50. Build an "ad copy performance database" — log every ad variant with its metrics for pattern recognition.

### 3.3 UX Writing & In-Product Copy

1. Write product copy that guides action: every string should help the user accomplish something.
2. Use clear, concise language: aim for a 6th-8th grade reading level — complexity kills comprehension.
3. Be consistent with terminology — use the same word for the same concept everywhere in the product.
4. Build a "product glossary" defining every term used in the UI — shared between design, engineering, and copywriting.
5. Write error messages that explain what happened, why, and what to do next — never just "Error."
6. Use progressive disclosure in copy — show only the information needed at each step.
7. Write button labels with specific actions: "Save changes" not "OK", "Delete project" not "Confirm."
8. Create onboarding copy that celebrates small wins and builds momentum toward the "aha moment."
9. Use "empty state" copy to guide users: when a feature is unused, explain its value and prompt action.
10. Write confirmation dialogues that clearly state the consequences: "Deleting this project will remove all files permanently."
11. Build a "tone map" that varies tone by context: celebratory for success, empathetic for errors, neutral for routine.
12. Use "microcopy" to reduce friction at decision points: "No credit card required", "Cancel anytime", "Takes 30 seconds."
13. Write tooltip and help text that adds context without cluttering the UI.
14. Create a "content patterns" library: standard patterns for notifications, alerts, confirmations, prompts, and empty states.
15. Use "voice and tone" guidelines specific to in-product contexts — marketing tone ≠ product tone.
16. Write loading and waiting states that set expectations: progress bars, time estimates, or engaging placeholder content.
17. Build a UX writing review process: copy review as part of design review, not an afterthought.
18. Use "actionable" notifications: "You have 3 unread messages — View now" not "3 new notifications."
19. Write permission requests that explain the benefit: "Enable notifications to get alerted when your report is ready."
20. Create a "content checklist" for new features: all strings, error states, empty states, success messages, and help text.
21. Use "plain language" principles: active voice, common words, short sentences, direct instructions.
22. Write placeholder text (input fields) that shows format and example: "e.g., name@company.com" not "Enter email."
23. Build internationalization (i18n) readiness into all copy: avoid idioms, allow for text expansion, use variables for dynamic content.
24. Use "inclusive language" — avoid gendered terms, cultural assumptions, and ableist language.
25. Write "upgrade/paywall" copy that emphasizes value gained, not features restricted.
26. Create a "string management" system — never hardcode copy in source code; use localization keys.
27. Use "success messages" that reinforce what the user accomplished: "Your profile is updated! Your changes are live."
28. Write "zero-state" messages for new users that introduce features without overwhelming.
29. Build a UX writing "content audit" into every major product redesign or feature update.
30. Use "A/B testing" on in-product copy — small copy changes can move conversion metrics significantly.
31. Write "system status" messages that keep users informed: "Syncing... this may take a moment."
32. Create "helper text" for complex forms — explain why you're asking for each piece of information.
33. Use "contextual help" — surface information where and when users need it, not in a separate help center.
34. Write "churn prevention" copy: re-engagement messages, value reminders, and "before you go" interstitials.
35. Build a "copy review" process with product, design, and engineering stakeholders.
36. Use "encouraging" copy at difficult moments: onboarding complexity, error recovery, long workflows.
37. Write "fallback" copy for edge cases: what happens when data is unavailable, a feature is down, or a request fails?
38. Create "content templates" for repeatable patterns: toast notifications, modals, banners, tooltips.
39. Use "conversational UI" copy for chat interfaces, wizards, and guided flows — natural language over formal instructions.
40. Write "accessibility-first" copy: meaningful link text ("View your report" not "Click here"), descriptive alt text, and ARIA labels.
41. Build a "copy debt" tracker — catalog all in-product copy that needs improvement and prioritize updates.
42. Use "cognitive load" principles: reduce choices, simplify language, and guide attention with hierarchy.
43. Write "contextual onboarding" — introduce features when users first encounter them, not in an upfront tutorial.
44. Create a "voice chart" mapping tone to user emotion: frustrated → empathetic, successful → celebratory, confused → clear.
45. Use "trust signals" in copy: security badges near payment flows, privacy language near data collection.
46. Write "feature discovery" copy that introduces new capabilities without disrupting the current workflow.
47. Build "content quality metrics" — track task completion rates, support ticket volume, and user satisfaction by feature.
48. Use "chunking" to break complex information into digestible segments — numbered steps, progressive disclosure.
49. Write "delight" moments — surprising, clever copy in unexpected places that strengthens brand personality.
50. Create a "UX writing playbook" documenting all patterns, principles, and processes for the team.

### 3.4 Long-Form Editorial / Blogging

1. Choose topics based on the intersection of: what your audience searches for, what you have expertise in, and what drives business goals.
2. Write compelling introductions: hook with a surprising stat, bold claim, or relatable scenario in the first 2 sentences.
3. Use the "inverted pyramid" for informational content: most important information first, details later.
4. Create "definitive guides" (3,000-5,000+ words) for high-value keywords — comprehensive coverage wins in search.
5. Build a "content brief" before writing: target keyword, search intent, outline, competitor analysis, unique angle.
6. Use subheadings (H2, H3) that are scannable and keyword-rich — readers and search engines both benefit.
7. Include original data, research, or insights that can't be found elsewhere — this is your competitive moat.
8. Write with "E-E-A-T" in mind: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness.
9. Use internal links strategically: link to related content, product pages, and conversion points naturally.
10. Include external links to authoritative sources — it builds credibility and signals thoroughness.
11. Optimize for featured snippets: use question-based H2s followed by concise 40-60 word answers.
12. Create "TL;DR" summaries or key takeaway boxes for long articles — respect readers' time.
13. Use images, charts, and visuals to break up text and illustrate complex points.
14. Write for a specific reading level: B2B SaaS typically targets 9th-10th grade; consumer content targets 6th-8th grade.
15. Build a "content refresh" schedule — update top-performing articles at least every 6-12 months.
16. Use "storytelling" frameworks in business content: challenge → journey → resolution → lesson.
17. Create "comparison articles" and "alternative" posts targeting bottom-of-funnel search queries.
18. Write strong "calls to action" within and at the end of every article — don't let readers leave without a next step.
19. Use "content upgrades" (downloadable resources) within articles to capture email addresses.
20. Build "topical clusters" — a pillar article linking to and from 5-15 supporting articles on subtopics.
21. Include author bios with credentials, photos, and links — authorship signals trust.
22. Use "jump links" (table of contents) for articles over 2,000 words — they improve UX and can appear in SERPs.
23. Write "listicles" when appropriate — numbered lists consistently drive high engagement and social shares.
24. Create "case study" blog posts with quantified results: "How [Company] achieved [X%] improvement in [metric]."
25. Use "original quotes" from industry experts, customers, or internal SMEs to add credibility and unique perspectives.
26. Build a "blog style guide" covering formatting, citation standards, image requirements, and SEO checklist.
27. Implement "content serialization" — multi-part series create return visits and anticipation.
28. Write "timely + timeless" content: tie trending topics to evergreen concepts for both immediate and lasting value.
29. Use "data journalism" techniques: analyze public data sets and present original insights.
30. Create "roundup" posts featuring expert opinions on industry topics — they're easy to produce and attract social shares.
31. Build a "contributor program" — invite customers, partners, and industry experts to write guest posts.
32. Use "content scoring" metrics: time on page, scroll depth, conversion rate, organic traffic, and social shares.
33. Write "update posts" when information changes: "Updated for 2026" signals freshness to readers and search engines.
34. Create "how-to" content with step-by-step instructions, screenshots, and practical examples.
35. Build a "blog editorial board" — cross-functional input on topics, angles, and priorities.
36. Use "long-tail keywords" in blog content — they're less competitive and capture specific search intent.
37. Write "opinionated" content that takes a clear position — wishy-washy content doesn't build authority or engagement.
38. Create "resource hub" pages that curate and organize blog content by topic for easy navigation.
39. Implement "content promotion" for every post: social distribution, email newsletter, internal sharing, community posting.
40. Build "content partnerships" for distribution: syndicate to relevant publications, newsletters, and communities.
41. Use "analytics-driven editing" — revise articles based on performance data (high bounce rate = weak intro; low scroll = weak middle).
42. Write "FAQ" sections within articles — they add long-tail keyword coverage and can appear as rich results.

47. Create "visual essays" that combine infographics, charts, and narrative for complex topics.
48. Implement "content recycling" — transform old blog posts into new formats (video, podcast, infographic).

### 3.6 Short-Form Social Video Creation

5. Write text overlays that work without sound — assume 85%+ viewers will watch silently.
6. Build a "content format library" of repeatable short-form formats: tutorials, reactions, before/after, myth-busting.

### 4.5 Conversion Rate Optimization (CRO)

1. Build a CRO practice grounded in data: analytics → hypotheses → experiments → results → learnings.
2. Map the full conversion funnel and identify the biggest leaks — optimize the highest-impact step first.
3. Use the "PIE" framework (Potential, Importance, Ease) to prioritize CRO experiments.
4. Start with qualitative research: heatmaps, session recordings, user surveys, and user testing before forming hypotheses.
5. Write experiment hypotheses in the format: "If we [change], then [metric] will [improve] because [reason]."
6. Use proper sample sizes and statistical significance (95% confidence minimum) — don't call tests early.
7. Run one test per page element at a time — isolate variables for clean results.
8. Build a "test backlog" organized by page, funnel stage, and priority — always have the next test ready.
9. Optimize above-the-fold content first — the hero section drives the majority of first impressions and exits.
10. Test headlines relentlessly — they're the highest-leverage copy element on any page.
11. Reduce form fields to the minimum required — every additional field reduces conversion by 5-10%.
12. Add social proof near CTAs: testimonials, user counts, ratings, logos, and trust badges.
13. Use "directional cues" — arrows, images of people looking toward the CTA, and layout flow.
14. Test CTA button color, copy, size, and placement — these small changes can move conversion significantly.
15. Implement "exit-intent" overlays with compelling offers — capture leaving visitors.
16. Build "landing page variants" for different traffic sources — personalize the experience by referral context.
17. Use "urgency" and "scarcity" ethically: countdown timers, limited availability, expiring offers.
18. Test long-form vs. short-form pages — more complex products often need more information, not less.
19. Implement "multi-step forms" — breaking long forms into steps improves completion rates.
20. Build a "CRO dashboard" tracking: test velocity, win rate, cumulative lift, and revenue impact.
21. Use "personalization" in CRO — dynamic content based on user segment, behavior, or referral source.
22. Test pricing page layouts: plan comparison tables, feature lists, recommended plans, and annual/monthly toggles.
23. Implement "trust signals" throughout the funnel: security badges, money-back guarantees, privacy policies.
24. Build "micro-conversions" tracking: scroll depth, video plays, CTA hover, tab switches — leading indicators of macro conversion.
25. Use "cognitive bias" frameworks in CRO: anchoring, loss aversion, social proof, reciprocity, default bias.
26. Test page speed impact on conversion — every additional second of load time reduces conversion by ~7%.
27. Implement "chatbots" and "live chat" on high-intent pages — real-time assistance reduces bounce.
28. Build a "winner archive" — document every winning test with hypothesis, variant, result, and implementation details.
29. Use "holdout groups" for major changes — measure true incremental impact over time.
30. Test "risk reversal" offers: free trials, money-back guarantees, "cancel anytime" — reduce purchase anxiety.

### 5.3 UX Design

10. Build "design patterns" that are consistent across the product — users shouldn't have to relearn interactions.
11. Use "progressive onboarding" — teach users in context, not through front-loaded tutorials.
12. Design "empty states" that guide users — the first screen a user sees should prompt meaningful action.

14. Use "information hierarchy" — size, color, contrast, and position should communicate importance.
15. Design "forgiving" interfaces — make it easy to undo, go back, and recover from mistakes.
16. Build "loading states" that communicate progress — skeletons, progress bars, and status messages reduce perceived wait time.
17. Use "recognition over recall" — show options rather than requiring users to remember from prior screens.
18. Design "confirmation dialogues" only for destructive or irreversible actions — not for routine operations.
19. Build "feedback mechanisms" into every interaction — visual, auditory, or haptic confirmation of user actions.
20. Use "proximity" and "grouping" (Gestalt principles) to show relationships between related elements.
21. Design "default values" and "smart suggestions" — reduce cognitive load by pre-filling where possible.
22. Build "navigation" that reflects user mental models, not organizational structure.
23. Use "contrast" for accessibility — minimum 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text.
24. Design "search" as a first-class feature — users often prefer search over navigation.
25. Build "multi-device" experiences — users switch devices; ensure continuity.
26. Use "real content" in designs, not lorem ipsum — real content reveals design problems early.
27. Design "error prevention" first, "error recovery" second — don't let users make mistakes if possible.
28. Build "notification design" that respects attention — not every event needs a notification.
29. Use "whitespace" deliberately — it improves readability, reduces cognitive load, and increases visual appeal.
30. Design "keyboard navigation" and "screen reader" compatibility from the start.
31. Build "design documentation" for engineering handoff — specifications, behaviors, edge cases, and interactions.
32. Use "data visualization" best practices — appropriate chart types, clear labels, accessible colors.
33. Design "settings" with sensible defaults — most users never change settings.
34. Build "offline states" and "poor connectivity" experiences — graceful degradation, not broken interfaces.
35. Use "motion" purposefully — animation should inform, not just decorate.
36. Design "permissions" and "consent" flows that are clear, transparent, and respectful.
37. Build "scalable" designs — will this work with 10 items? 1,000? 100,000?
38. Use "user testing" throughout the design process — not just at the end.

13. Design "gestures" that are discoverable — swipe, pinch, rotate should have visual hints or onboarding.
14. Use "spring physics" for natural-feeling interactions — they mimic real-world object behavior.
15. Build "undo" mechanisms for destructive actions — "Deleted. Undo?" with a timer.
16. Design "modal" interactions with proper focus trapping and keyboard navigation.
17. Use "progress indicators" for multi-step flows — show users where they are and what's next.
18. Build "toast notifications" that auto-dismiss, are non-blocking, and provide action options.

20. Use "parallax" and "scroll-linked" animations sparingly — they add polish but can cause performance issues.
21. Build "collapsible/expandable" patterns with smooth height transitions and clear toggle affordances.
22. Design "cursor" changes that communicate interactivity — pointer for clickable, grab for draggable.
23. Use "haptic feedback" on mobile for important actions — confirmation, error, and success states.

### 5.6 Information Architecture

# PART C: ORCHESTRATOR ARCHITECTURE

---

## Orchestrator "Org Chart" Best Practices

---

### Chief Orchestrator Agent

1. Route user requests to the appropriate Virtual CMO or Virtual CTO based on intent classification.
2. Maintain conversation context across specialist agent interactions — the user talks to one entity, not many.
3. Implement "intent detection" — classify requests as marketing, technical, or cross-functional.
4. Build "memory management" — maintain user preferences, project context, and historical decisions.
5. Implement "conflict resolution" — when marketing and tech agents recommend conflicting approaches.
6. Build "priority management" — sequence multi-step requests in the optimal order.
7. Maintain a "project state" — track what's been done, what's in progress, and what's next.
8. Implement "escalation logic" — when a specialist agent can't handle a request, escalate to the chief.
9. Build "cross-functional coordination" — some tasks require both marketing and tech agents working together.
10. Maintain "quality gates" — review specialist agent outputs before presenting to the user.

---

### Virtual CMO (Head of Marketing Agents)

1. Route marketing requests to the correct specialist agent based on task type and funnel stage.
2. Maintain "campaign context" — track active campaigns, brand guidelines, and messaging across all marketing agents.
3. Implement "brand consistency checks" — ensure all marketing outputs align with brand guidelines.
4. Build "cross-channel coordination" — ensure marketing messages are consistent across email, social, ads, and content.
5. Maintain "performance awareness" — track aggregate marketing metrics and guide resource allocation.

---

### Virtual CTO (Head of Tech Agents)

1. Route technical requests to the correct specialist agent based on technology domain and task type.
2. Maintain "architecture context" — track tech stack decisions, design system state, and infrastructure configuration.
3. Implement "quality standards" — ensure all technical outputs meet code quality, accessibility, and performance standards.
4. Build "cross-domain coordination" — ensure front-end, back-end, and infrastructure changes are compatible.
5. Maintain "technical debt awareness" — track accumulated debt and recommend remediation priorities.
