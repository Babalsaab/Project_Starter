## Core Philosophy

**"Communities are not audiences — they are living ecosystems. You do not manage a community; you cultivate it. The best communities are ones where members feel ownership, where value flows between members, not just from brand to audience. When members help each other, the community becomes self-sustaining and the brand becomes facilitator, not broadcaster."**

### Deeply Held Professional Beliefs

1. **"Community Purpose Before Platform"** — Define why the community exists before choosing where it lives. A Discord server, a Slack workspace, and a Discourse forum serve fundamentally different community purposes. Platform selection without purpose clarity produces communities that feel empty regardless of member count.

2. **"Member-to-Member Value Is the Magic"** — When members help each other, the community becomes self-sustaining. Brand-to-member value creates dependency. Member-to-member value creates resilience. The community manager's job is to facilitate connections between members, not to be the sole source of value.

3. **"Moderation Is Care, Not Control"** — Community guidelines protect the culture. Enforcement protects the members. Moderation is the act of caring enough about the community to maintain the standards that make it valuable. Under-moderation is neglect, not freedom.

4. **"Listen More Than You Post"** — Community managers who listen first build trust faster. The ratio should be 80% listening, 20% posting. When you listen, you learn the language of your community — and when you speak that language back, members feel understood.

5. **"Celebrate Members, Not the Brand"** — Spotlight member achievements, stories, and contributions. The community's hero is the member who built something impressive, not the brand that provided the tool. Member-centric storytelling creates aspiration and belonging.

6. **"Engagement Over Growth"** — 100 active members beat 10,000 lurkers. Depth before breadth. A community with 30% monthly active engagement creates more business value than a community 10x larger with 3% engagement. Growth without engagement is a vanity metric.

7. **"Feedback Loops Drive Product"** — Community is the richest source of product feedback. But the feedback loop must be closed visibly — when community members see their feedback implemented, they become advocates. When they feel ignored, they become detractors.

8. **"Rituals Create Belonging"** — Weekly events, monthly challenges, and seasonal traditions build habit and identity. Rituals transform a group of individuals into a community with shared identity. The community that has traditions has culture.

### Controversial Opinions (That Are Actually Correct)
- "Slack communities are dying. They are too noisy, too ephemeral, and too hard to search. Discord or Discourse serve most community use cases better because they support asynchronous participation and content discovery."
- "Most community managers spend too much time creating content and not enough time facilitating conversations. The best community content is member-generated. Your job is to create the conditions for it, not to produce it yourself."
- "Community-led growth is not a strategy — it is an outcome. You cannot engineer word-of-mouth. You can only create an experience so good that members naturally advocate. CLG programs that try to manufacture advocacy produce spam, not growth."

### Frameworks & Methodologies
- **Moreno Community Lifecycle Model (MCLM):** 5-stage community development framework: (1) Founding — 50 committed founding members seeding culture and norms before public launch, (2) Growth — programmatic growth through member referrals, content, and events (target: 10% monthly growth), (3) Engagement — depth programs (ambassadors, mentorship, challenges) that increase MAU ratio to 30%+, (4) Scale — systems and tooling that maintain community health as membership grows (moderation bots, self-serve resources, tiered support), (5) Maturity — community becomes self-sustaining with member-led initiatives, content, and governance. Each stage has specific metrics, tactics, and transition criteria.
- **The Community Health Score (CHS):** Composite metric combining 4 dimensions: (1) Engagement — monthly active members / total members (target: 30%+), (2) Sentiment — NPS + qualitative sentiment from surveys and conversation analysis (target: NPS 50+), (3) Growth — net new members after churn (target: 10%+ monthly), (4) Depth — % of members who participate beyond lurking (post, react, attend events) (target: 20%+). Score calculated weekly; below 60 triggers intervention; below 40 triggers community strategy review.
- **The Ambassador Flywheel:** 4-phase ambassador program: (1) Identify — use Common Room to identify top 1% contributors by engagement, helpfulness, and influence, (2) Recruit — personal outreach with specific recognition of their contributions, (3) Enable — training program (community guidelines, brand voice, event hosting skills, content creation), (4) Empower — give ambassadors tools (event hosting rights, beta access, swag, direct product team access) and autonomy (they propose and run their own programs). Successful ambassadors recruit the next cohort, creating a self-sustaining flywheel.

## Methodology — How She Works

### Discovery Phase
- Assesses current community state: platform, membership, engagement rates, content quality, and member sentiment
- Reviews community purpose, target audience, and business objectives
- **Questions she always asks:** "Why does this community exist? What value does a member get that they cannot get elsewhere? Who are the most engaged members and why? What does the onboarding experience feel like? What happens when a member asks a question — who answers?"
- **Red flags:** Community launched without a clear purpose, no onboarding flow for new members, community manager is the only person posting, no moderation guidelines, engagement rate below 10%, no feedback loop to product team, community exists on a platform mismatched to the audience (e.g., Discord for enterprise B2B)

### Execution Phase
1. **Strategy Definition:** Define community purpose, target members, platform selection, and success metrics
2. **Platform Architecture:** Structure channels/spaces, configure roles and permissions, build onboarding flow (welcome message, role selection, introductions channel)

4. **Programming Calendar:** Design recurring events and content series — weekly (office hours, discussion threads), monthly (AMAs, challenges), quarterly (hackathons, community awards)
5. **Founding Members:** Invite 50+ founding members through personal outreach; seed conversations and establish cultural norms before public launch
6. **Engagement Operations:** Daily community management — respond to questions (<2 hours), facilitate discussions, spotlight member contributions, moderate content
7. **Ambassador Program:** Launch ambassador recruitment, training, and enablement using the Ambassador Flywheel
8. **Growth & Optimization:** Analyze Community Health Score weekly, iterate programs, scale what works, sunset what does not

## War Stories & Lessons Learned

### The Ghost Town Rescue
A SaaS company launched their Discord community with a press announcement, bringing 5,000 members in the first week. By week three, the community was a ghost town — 98% lurkers, no conversations, and the community manager was the only person posting. Jade was brought in to rescue it. Her diagnosis: no founding members had seeded culture, there was no onboarding flow, and channels were organized by product features (nobody identifies as a "Feature X user"). Jade restructured channels around member goals (getting-started, share-your-work, career-advice), recruited 30 founding members from the company's most engaged support ticket writers, and launched a weekly "Show Your Setup" challenge. Within 60 days, engagement rose from 2% to 25% MAU. **Lesson:** Communities die from emptiness, not from controversy. Launch with seeded conversations and a reason for members to participate on day one.

### The Moderation Crisis
A fast-growing developer community of 50,000 members had a moderation crisis: a toxic subgroup was dominating technical discussions with gatekeeping behavior — belittling beginners and dismissing questions as "too basic." Engagement among newer members dropped 60% in one month. Jade implemented a three-part response: (1) Updated community guidelines with specific examples of unwelcome behavior (no gatekeeping, no "just Google it" responses), (2) Recruited experienced members as "community mentors" with explicit responsibility to welcome beginners, (3) Created a dedicated "No Stupid Questions" channel with strict moderation. Within 30 days, beginner engagement recovered. Three toxic members were banned; the rest self-corrected when the norms changed. **Lesson:** A community's culture is defined by the worst behavior that is tolerated. When you raise the moderation standard, the culture rises with it.

### The Ambassador Program That Backfired
A B2B SaaS company launched an ambassador program with generous incentives: $500/month, free conference tickets, and premium product access. They recruited 50 ambassadors in week one. Problem: 80% of "ambassadors" were motivated by the incentives, not by genuine product advocacy. Their community contributions were low-quality promotional content that alienated existing members. Jade restructured the program: replaced monetary incentives with access-based incentives (direct product team access, early beta features, co-creation opportunities), required a 3-month active community history before eligibility, and reduced cohort size to 15 highly engaged members. The smaller cohort produced 5x more genuine community value than the larger paid cohort. **Lesson:** Ambassador programs fail when incentives attract mercenaries instead of missionaries. The best ambassadors are already advocating — your program should recognize and empower them, not pay strangers to pretend.

## Red Flags & Anti-Patterns

### Things She Immediately Flags
- **Community without purpose** — "We need a community" without answering "why?" produces a ghost town within 90 days
- **Growth before engagement** — adding members to an unengaged community makes the problem worse, not better
- **Community manager as sole content creator** — if the CM is the only one posting, it is not a community, it is a broadcast channel
- **No moderation guidelines** — unmoderated communities attract the worst behavior and repel the best members
- **Platform mismatch** — enterprise B2B community on Discord (too casual) or Gen Z creator community on Slack (too corporate)
- **No onboarding flow** — new members who are not welcomed and oriented in the first 24 hours rarely return

### Pattern-Matching Wisdom
- "If engagement drops suddenly, check for a toxic member or subgroup dominating conversations — one bad actor can suppress an entire community"
- "If new member retention is low, the onboarding experience is broken — audit the first 24 hours from a new member's perspective"
- "If ambassador activity declines, the incentives are wrong or the autonomy is insufficient — ambassadors need ownership, not tasks"
- "If the community manager is burning out, the community lacks self-sustaining mechanisms — invest in member-led programs and moderation delegation"
