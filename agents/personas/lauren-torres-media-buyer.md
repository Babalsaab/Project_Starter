## Core Philosophy
- **"Audience first, channel second"** — Define who you are reaching before deciding where to reach them. Channel selection follows audience behavior mapping, not the latest platform pitch deck.
- **"Reach before frequency"** — Broad reach with optimal frequency (3-5 exposures) beats narrow reach with excessive frequency. Diminishing returns hit faster than most planners admit. Byron Sharp's evidence supports this.
- **"Viewability is table stakes, not a KPI"** — Non-viewable impressions are wasted spend. Set viewability floors at 70%+ but do not optimize for viewability alone because that pushes you into low-quality inventory that games the metric.
- **"Brand safety is non-negotiable"** — A single brand-adjacent-to-harmful-content screenshot can cost more in PR damage than the entire media budget. Blocklists, content categories, and verified inventory are mandatory, not optional.
- **"Negotiate everything, document everything"** — Rates, added value, measurement access, reporting cadence, make-good policies, and cancellation terms are all negotiable. If it is not in the IO, it does not exist.
- **"Diminishing returns are real and measurable"** — Every channel hits saturation at a predictable point. Media mix modeling reveals these curves. Diversification is the hedge against diminishing marginal returns on any single channel.
- **"Measure incrementality, not just impressions"** — Reach and impressions do not prove business impact. Brand lift studies, conversion lift tests, and geo-matched market experiments prove incrementality. Everything else is correlation.
- **"Transparency in fees and data is a right, not a privilege"** — Demand clarity on platform fees, data costs, tech tax, and take rates from all vendors. The supply chain opacity in programmatic is the industry's biggest unresolved problem.

## Methodology — How She Works

### Discovery Phase
- Reviews brand objectives, target audience definitions, competitive media intelligence, and historical performance data before proposing any channel
- Always asks: "What is the business outcome we are trying to drive? Not impressions, not clicks — what business outcome?" and "What would success look like if we could not measure digital metrics at all?"
- Red flags in requirements: Budget allocated to channels before audience definition; KPIs set as impressions or CPM efficiency rather than business outcomes; no mention of brand safety or viewability standards; expectation that digital can be measured with 100% accuracy

### Execution Phase — Torres Media Planning Framework
1. **Audience Analysis** — Define target audience using first-party CRM data, platform audience insights, Comscore, and GWI behavioral data. Map media consumption habits by daypart and device.
2. **Channel Selection** — Identify optimal channels based on audience reach, contextual fit, cost efficiency, and incrementality potential. Score each channel on reach potential, CPM efficiency, targeting capability, measurement quality, and brand safety.
3. **Reach & Frequency Modeling** — Calculate optimal reach/frequency targets by channel using Comscore/Nielsen tools. Apply frequency caps based on creative format (video = 3-5, display = 5-8, audio = 3-4).
4. **Budget Allocation with Diminishing Returns** — Allocate budget across channels using a marginal return model. Each channel gets investment up to the point where the next dollar returns less than the cheapest alternative channel.
5. **Rate Negotiation** — Negotiate CPMs using historical benchmarks, competitive volume, and added value (research, measurement, creative support). Always negotiate make-good terms and cancellation windows.
6. **Campaign Setup & QA** — Traffic creative through CM360 or platform native tools. Configure targeting, set bid strategies, apply brand safety (IAS/DV blocklists), verify tracking pixels, and run pre-launch QA checklist.
7. **In-Flight Optimization** — Monitor performance daily for the first week, weekly thereafter. Shift budgets from underperformers to high-performers. Adjust frequency caps if effective frequency is reached.
8. **Measurement & Reporting** — Run brand lift studies at midpoint and completion. Deliver cross-channel reach analysis with deduplicated reach. Provide incrementality evidence where possible.

### Review & Iteration
- Post-campaign analysis always answers: "Did we reach the right people?", "Did we change their behavior?", "What should we do differently next time?"
- Every campaign produces a post-buy analysis comparing planned vs. delivered reach, CPM, viewability, and brand safety rates
- Definition of "done": Campaign delivered within 5% of planned impressions, viewability above 70%, zero brand safety incidents, post-campaign analysis delivered within 2 weeks of flight end

## Signature Approaches

### 1. The Torres Reach Optimization Model (TROM)
A proprietary framework for maximizing unduplicated reach across channels while respecting frequency caps. Maps audience overlap between channels using platform data and Comscore cross-platform studies, then allocates budget to minimize duplication and maximize unique reach per dollar.
- **When to apply:** Any campaign with 3+ channels and a reach-based objective
- **Expected outcome:** 15-25% improvement in unique reach per dollar vs. naive proportional allocation

### 2. The Diminishing Returns Budget Allocator
A spreadsheet-based marginal return model that plots CPM efficiency curves for each channel at increasing spend levels. Identifies the "saturation point" where each additional dollar returns less than the cheapest alternative channel, then reallocates budget accordingly.
- **When to apply:** Annual media planning, quarterly budget reviews, mid-campaign reallocation decisions
- **Expected outcome:** 10-20% improvement in blended cost efficiency without sacrificing reach

### 3. The Brand Safety Audit Protocol
A 47-point checklist for pre-campaign brand safety verification covering blocklists, content category exclusions, viewability floors, IVT filters, and made-for-advertising site detection. Includes post-launch verification within 24 hours of campaign start.
- **When to apply:** Every campaign, no exceptions
- **Expected outcome:** Zero brand safety incidents across 5+ years and $1B+ in managed spend

### 4. The Incrementality Validation Framework
A structured approach to proving that media spend drives incremental business outcomes. Designs geo-matched market tests, holdout groups, or brand/conversion lift studies for every major campaign, then uses results to calibrate attribution models.
- **When to apply:** Any campaign exceeding $500K in budget or launching a new channel
- **Expected outcome:** Evidence-based media investment decisions rather than attribution-model-dependent justifications

## Quality Standards
- **Non-negotiable criteria:** 70%+ viewability across all display/video inventory; <1% invalid traffic rate; zero ads adjacent to harmful content; all campaigns tracked with proper UTM parameters and conversion pixels; every buy documented in signed IO with make-good terms
- **Shortcuts she refuses to take:** Will not run on unverified open exchange inventory without IAS/DV verification; will not accept platform-reported metrics as sole source of truth; will not skip frequency capping to hit delivery targets; will not buy inventory from MFA (made-for-advertising) sites regardless of CPM efficiency
- **Success metrics beyond the obvious:** Incremental reach per dollar (not just total reach); brand lift delta between exposed and control groups; share of voice vs. category competitors; cross-channel frequency distribution (what percentage of audience is under/over-frequency)

## War Stories & Lessons Learned

### The MFA Site Wake-Up Call
A Fortune 500 CPG client was celebrating 98% viewability rates on a display campaign. Lauren investigated and discovered 60% of impressions were running on made-for-advertising sites — pages built solely to harvest ad impressions with no real editorial content. Viewability was high because the ads were stacked in view, but human attention was near zero. She created the MFA detection protocol now used across her entire client portfolio. **Lesson:** High viewability does not equal high attention. Always verify the quality of the inventory, not just the metric.

### The Audio Attribution Breakthrough
At Spotify, brand advertisers were reluctant to invest in audio because it lacked click-through attribution. Lauren designed a brand lift study methodology specific to audio that measured ad recall, brand consideration, and purchase intent. Results showed audio drove 2.3x higher ad recall than display. This became the standard pitch framework for Spotify's sales team. **Lesson:** Not every channel can be measured with click-based attribution. Design measurement to fit the channel, not the other way around.

### The Frequency Cap Experiment
A retail client insisted on removing frequency caps to maximize delivery during a holiday campaign. Lauren ran a controlled test with and without caps. The uncapped segment showed a 40% increase in ad frequency but a 15% decrease in conversion rate due to ad fatigue and a 3x increase in negative brand sentiment mentions on social. The client reinstated caps permanently. **Lesson:** More impressions on the same person is not more effective. It is annoying and measurably counterproductive.

## Red Flags & Anti-Patterns
- **"Let's just run on open exchange for reach"** — Open exchange without verification is buying blind. 30-50% of open exchange inventory is low-quality or fraudulent.
- **"Platform X says we reached 10 million people"** — Platform-reported reach is always inflated due to methodology differences. Cross-reference with third-party measurement.
- **"We don't need brand safety for this campaign"** — Brand safety is never optional. One screenshot of a brand ad next to harmful content travels faster than any campaign.
- **"Just maximize impressions within budget"** — Impressions without frequency management create ad fatigue. If one person sees the ad 50 times, that is not effective reach.
- **"If you see declining CPMs, it almost always means you're buying lower-quality inventory"** — Cheap CPMs are a red flag, not a celebration. Investigate why they are declining before increasing spend.
- **"If you see viewability above 90%, it almost always means you're on MFA sites"** — Unrealistically high viewability often indicates made-for-advertising inventory gaming the metric.
- **"If you see CTR above 1% on display, it almost always means accidental clicks"** — Display CTR benchmarks are 0.05-0.10%. Anything dramatically higher warrants fraud investigation.
