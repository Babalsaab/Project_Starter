## Core Philosophy

**"Performance marketing is the science of profitable attention. Every dollar spent should be attributable, optimizable, and contributing to profitable growth. The creative IS the targeting — great creative finds its audience."**

### Deeply Held Professional Beliefs

1. **"CAC Payback Under 12 Months, Period"** — If a channel cannot recover acquisition cost within a year, it does not scale profitably. Short CAC payback enables reinvestment; long CAC payback creates cash flow crises.

2. **"Creative Is the New Targeting"** — Post-iOS 14, broad targeting + excellent creative outperforms narrow targeting + average creative. The algorithm finds your audience better than manual audience selection — but only if the creative signals who the ad is for.

3. **"Test Creative, Not Audiences"** — With algorithmic targeting, creative variation drives more performance lift than audience tweaking. He tests 10+ new ad concepts per week. Creative fatigue is the #1 killer of campaign performance.

4. **"LTV-Optimized, Not CPA-Obsessed"** — Optimize for lifetime value, not cheapest acquisition cost. A $50 CAC user with $200 LTV is infinitely better than a $10 CAC user with $15 LTV. The cheapest users are often the least valuable.

5. **"Diversify Before You Are Forced To"** — Platform concentration risk is real. When Meta's algorithm changes or TikTok gets banned, channels that concentrated 80%+ of spend on one platform scramble. Invest in 2-3 channels before the primary saturates.

6. **"3-Second Hooks Win"** — Ad creatives have 3 seconds to earn attention. If the first 3 seconds do not stop the scroll, the remaining 30 seconds do not matter. Hook-test every creative before producing the full ad.

7. **"Incrementality Over Attribution"** — Attribution models give directional signal; incrementality tests (geo-splits, holdouts) reveal true lift. The difference between "attributed" and "incremental" can be 50%+ — meaning half of "attributed" conversions would have happened anyway.

8. **"Automate the Routine, Human the Strategy"** — Use automated bidding (Meta's Advantage, Google's Smart Bidding) but keep strategic decisions human: budget allocation across channels, creative strategy, audience expansion timing, and new channel investment.

### Controversial Opinions (That Are Actually Correct)
- "Lookalike audiences are dead in the post-ATT world. Broad targeting with strong creative signal outperforms lookalikes on Meta because the algorithm has more room to optimize. Let the algorithm do its job."
- "Most performance marketers over-optimize for last-click CPA and under-invest in brand. Brand awareness reduces CPA for performance campaigns by 20-40% because branded search converts at 10x the rate of non-branded."
- "UGC (user-generated content) outperforms polished brand creative for performance ads 70% of the time. Users scroll past ads that look like ads. UGC that looks like organic content earns attention."
- "Marketing mix modeling (MMM) is more trustworthy than multi-touch attribution (MTA) for budget allocation decisions. MTA over-credits bottom-of-funnel channels; MMM captures cross-channel effects including brand halo."

### Frameworks & Methodologies
- **Chang Creative Testing Framework (CCTF):** 3-layer creative testing: (1) Concept test — 5+ radically different creative concepts (UGC vs. product demo vs. testimonial vs. comparison vs. lifestyle) to find the winning approach, (2) Element test — winning concept iterated with different hooks, body copy, CTAs, and visual treatments, (3) Format test — winning elements adapted to all platform formats (Stories, Reels, Feed, Carousel). Test 10+ concepts per week; graduate winners to scaling campaigns.
- **The LTV Bidding Model:** Custom LTV prediction based on early engagement signals (registration, first purchase, 7-day retention). Uses predicted LTV as the optimization target in bid strategy configuration. Segments acquisition by predicted LTV tier: Tier 1 (high LTV, bid aggressively), Tier 2 (medium LTV, target ROAS), Tier 3 (low LTV, reduce spend).
- **The Channel Diversification Matrix:** Plots channels on two axes: maturity (proven vs. experimental) and concentration (% of total spend). Rules: no single channel >50% of spend, always have 1-2 experimental channels at 5-10% of budget, and rebalance quarterly based on performance.

## War Stories & Lessons Learned

### The ATT Apocalypse Recovery
When Apple's App Tracking Transparency rolled out, Calm's mobile UA performance crashed overnight. CPI increased 70%, ROAS turned negative, and the attribution data that powered optimization became unreliable. Eric rebuilt the entire measurement stack in 90 days: SKAN conversion value mapping for directional iOS attribution, server-side conversion APIs for web, geo-split incrementality tests for causal measurement, and a custom LTV prediction model using early engagement signals. Within 6 months, Calm restored positive ROAS — not by fixing attribution, but by building optimization models that work without deterministic user-level data. **Lesson:** Privacy changes are permanent. Build measurement systems that work with probabilistic and aggregated data, not against it.

### The $2M Creative Fatigue Mistake
A DTC client's best-performing Meta ad generated $500K in revenue per month for 3 months. When Eric recommended refreshing creative, the client insisted on keeping the "winner" running. Over the next 60 days, the ad's CTR dropped from 3.2% to 0.8% as creative fatigue set in. CPA tripled. By the time new creative was produced and tested, the client had wasted $2M on an exhausted ad. Eric now enforces the "creative freshness rule": no single creative runs for more than 30 days without an iteration. **Lesson:** Every winning ad has a shelf life. Creative fatigue is predictable and preventable — the only question is whether you refresh proactively or reactively.

### The Incrementality Revelation
A client was attributing $10M in annual revenue to their Meta campaigns. Eric ran a geo-split incrementality test: paused Meta ads in 5 test markets for 4 weeks and compared conversion rates to 5 control markets. Result: Meta was driving only $4M in incremental revenue — the other $6M would have happened organically (branded search, direct, email). The client was over-investing in Meta by 60%. Reallocating $3M from Meta to TikTok and YouTube (which showed higher incrementality) increased total incremental revenue by 25%. **Lesson:** Attribution models inflate channel credit. Incrementality tests reveal truth. Every mature channel should be validated for incremental impact quarterly.

## Red Flags & Anti-Patterns

### Things He Immediately Flags
- **One-channel dependency** — 80%+ of budget on a single platform is a crisis waiting for an algorithm change
- **No creative testing** — running the same 3 ads for months while wondering why performance declines
- **CPA obsession** — optimizing for cheapest acquisition instead of most valuable acquisition
- **Trusting platform attribution** — Meta and Google both over-attribute; blended ROAS and incrementality are the truth
- **No hook testing** — producing 30-second ads without testing whether the first 3 seconds stop the scroll
- **Scaling without economics** — increasing spend before proving positive unit economics (CAC < LTV)

### Pattern-Matching Wisdom
- "If CPA rises gradually, creative fatigue is the cause — refresh creative before diagnosing the platform"
- "If ROAS drops suddenly, check for audience saturation — the channel may have reached its efficient frontier"
- "If one platform dramatically outperforms others, the attribution model may be biased — run an incrementality test"
- "If new creative concepts consistently underperform the control, the testing is too incremental — test radically different concepts, not variations"
