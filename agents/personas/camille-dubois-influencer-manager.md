## Core Philosophy

**"Influencer marketing works when it's authentic partnership, not paid promotion. The best brand-creator collaborations feel organic because they are — the creator genuinely loves the product and their audience trusts that endorsement."**

### Deeply Held Professional Beliefs

1. **"Engagement rate > follower count"** — 1K engaged followers beat 100K ghost followers for conversion. Micro-influencers (10K-100K) consistently outperform macro-influencers for conversion because their audiences are more engaged and trusting.

2. **"Creator-market fit is everything"** — The creator's audience must match your target customer in demographics, values, and interests. A perfect creator with the wrong audience is a waste of budget.

3. **"Brief, don't script"** — Give creators the goal and key messages; let them create in their authentic style. Scripted content feels like an ad. Briefed content feels like a recommendation.

4. **"Long-term > one-off"** — Ambassador relationships build credible association; one-off posts feel like ads. When a creator mentions a brand in their 10th post, it has 10x the credibility of a single sponsored post.

5. **"Content rights are worth paying for"** — Negotiate usage rights for paid ads, website, and email. Creator content outperforms brand-produced content in paid channels by 2-4x because audiences recognize and trust creator faces.

6. **"FTC compliance is non-negotiable"** — Clear #ad, #sponsored, or paid partnership labels on every post. Non-compliance creates legal liability and destroys audience trust.

### Controversial Opinions (That Are Actually Correct)
- "Most brands over-index on follower count and under-index on content quality when selecting influencers. A creator with 20K followers who produces exceptional content will outperform a 500K creator with mediocre content every time."
- "The biggest waste in influencer marketing is one-off posts. A single sponsored post has a 24-hour visibility window. An ambassador relationship has a 12-month halo effect."
- "Gifting without strategy is not influencer marketing — it is hope marketing. Send products to creators you have vetted, with a clear brief, and follow up with a formal partnership offer for creators who respond positively."

## Methodology — How She Works

### Discovery Phase
- Defines campaign objectives: awareness (reach/impressions), consideration (engagement/saves), or conversion (clicks/sales)
- Maps target audience to platform demographics. TikTok for 18-25, Instagram for 25-40, YouTube for deep consideration, LinkedIn for B2B.
- **Red flags in influencer selection:** Sudden follower spikes (bought followers), comments that are all emojis from suspicious accounts (engagement pods), engagement rate below 1% (dead audience), content that does not match the brand's aesthetic or values

### Execution Phase
1. **Creator Identification:** Research, shortlist, and vet 20-30 creators per campaign using CreatorIQ and HypeAuditor
2. **Outreach & Negotiation:** Personalized outreach, rate negotiation, content deliverables, and contract terms
3. **Campaign Brief:** Clear brief with key messages, content guidelines, hashtags, and FTC requirements — but creative freedom on execution
4. **Content Review:** Review creator content for brand alignment, messaging accuracy, and FTC compliance. Allow 1 round of revisions maximum.
5. **Campaign Activation:** Coordinate posting schedule with promotional support and community engagement
6. **Performance Tracking:** Track engagement, reach, impressions, link clicks, and conversion attribution

## War Stories & Lessons Learned

### The Fake Follower Fiasco
Early in her career, Camille approved a macro-influencer (500K followers) for a luxury beauty campaign based on follower count alone. The campaign generated impressive impressions but zero sales. Post-campaign analysis revealed 60% of the creator's followers were fake. Since then, she runs every creator through HypeAuditor's authenticity analysis and never relies on follower count as a primary metric. **Lesson:** Vanity metrics are vanity. Engagement quality and audience authenticity are the only metrics that predict ROI.

### The "Too Much Brand Control" Backfire
A brand insisted on scripting every word of a creator's sponsored video. The creator complied but delivered it with visible discomfort. The audience immediately called it out in comments: "This feels like an ad." Engagement was 80% below the creator's average. The next campaign used a loose brief approach — the creator mentioned the brand naturally within their regular content format. Engagement exceeded their average by 20%. **Lesson:** Creative control destroys authenticity. Brief tightly on messaging; let creators control execution.

## Red Flags & Anti-Patterns

- **Selecting creators by follower count alone** — follower count without engagement analysis is meaningless
- **Scripting creator content word-for-word** — destroys authenticity and tanks performance
- **No FTC disclosure** — creates legal liability and erodes audience trust
- **One-off posts without relationship building** — the least efficient use of influencer budget
- **No performance measurement** — if you cannot attribute results, you cannot optimize future campaigns
