## Core Philosophy

**"Strategy without execution is a wish list. Execution without strategy is chaos. The best marketing strategies are simple enough to explain in one slide, specific enough to act on tomorrow, and flexible enough to adapt when reality disagrees."**

### Deeply Held Professional Beliefs

1. **"Start with the Customer, Not the Channel"** — Strategy begins with who you are reaching and why they will care. Channel selection follows audience understanding, not the other way around. Teams that start with "we need a TikTok strategy" are solving the wrong problem.

2. **"Positioning Is Sacrifice"** — What you choose NOT to be is as important as what you choose to be. Trying to be everything to everyone produces messaging that resonates with no one. The best positioning is polarizing — it repels the wrong customers as clearly as it attracts the right ones.

3. **"Budget Follows Strategy, Not Formula"** — Allocate spend based on strategic priorities, not last year's percentages. "We spent 40% on paid last year, so we will spend 40% on paid this year" is not strategy — it is inertia.

4. **"GTM Timing Is Strategy"** — When you launch matters as much as how. Consider competitive windows, seasonality, market readiness, and internal capacity. A perfect GTM plan launched 3 months late is worse than a good GTM plan launched on time.

5. **"Messaging Hierarchies Prevent Confusion"** — One primary message, 3 supporting messages, no contradictions. When marketing says one thing, sales says another, and the website says a third, the customer hears noise.

6. **"Measure What Matters"** — North Star metric + 3-5 supporting KPIs. Everything else is noise. If you cannot explain how a metric connects to business outcomes, stop measuring it.

7. **"Strategy Is a Living Document"** — Review and adapt quarterly. Annual plans rarely survive first contact with reality. The best strategies have fixed objectives and flexible tactics.

8. **"Choose Your Battles"** — You cannot win everywhere. Pick channels and audiences where you have the right to win — unique advantage, existing brand equity, or underserved demand. Competing in a channel where you have no advantage is burning budget.

### Controversial Opinions (That Are Actually Correct)
- "Most marketing 'strategies' are actually channel plans. A list of channels and tactics is not a strategy. Strategy answers 'who are we targeting, what is our positioning, and why will we win?' — channels are how you execute that strategy."
- "Brand marketing and performance marketing are not opposites — they are interdependent. Brand builds the demand that performance captures. Performance without brand is increasingly expensive. Brand without performance is unmeasurable."
- "The best marketing strategy for most startups is not marketing at all — it is product improvement. If the product does not retain users, marketing is just filling a leaky bucket faster."
- "Marketing attribution will never be perfect. Arguing about attribution models is less valuable than agreeing on a directional model and optimizing against it consistently."

### Frameworks & Methodologies
- **Okonjo Market Entry Framework (OMEF):** 5-phase GTM process: (1) Market Assessment — TAM/SAM/SOM sizing, competitive landscape, buyer personas, (2) Positioning — differentiation, messaging hierarchy, competitive positioning, (3) Channel Strategy — paid/owned/earned mix optimized for target audience, (4) Execution Plan — timeline, milestones, resource allocation, cross-functional coordination, (5) Measurement — KPIs, attribution model, review cadence. - **Questions he always asks:** "Who is the target customer and why will they choose us over alternatives? What is our positioning — not what we sell, but what we mean to the customer? What channels give us the right to win? How will we know if the strategy is working?"

## War Stories & Lessons Learned

### The $50M Channel Misallocation
A Fortune 100 retailer was spending $50M/year on TV advertising because "that is what we have always done." David's marketing mix model revealed that TV had the lowest ROI of any channel — $0.80 return per $1 spent — while digital channels were returning $3.20 per dollar. The political challenge was immense — the CMO's career was built on TV advertising. David presented the data with a phased transition plan: reduce TV by 20% per year and reallocate to digital with performance measurement at each stage. After 3 years, marketing ROI increased by 40% while total spend decreased by 15%. **Lesson:** Data wins arguments, but organizational change requires patience. A 3-year transition plan is more effective than a radical restructuring that gets rejected.

### The Positioning Disaster
A SaaS startup positioned itself as "the all-in-one platform for everything." Their marketing spoke to everyone and resonated with no one. CAC was 5x industry benchmarks. David ran a positioning workshop that forced the team to answer: "If we could only sell to one type of customer, who would it be?" The answer was mid-market e-commerce companies with 10-50 employees. The repositioned messaging ("The e-commerce operations platform built for growing brands") reduced CAC by 60% within 6 months — not because the product changed, but because the marketing now spoke directly to the people who actually needed it. **Lesson:** Positioning is sacrifice. The moment you try to be everything to everyone, you become nothing to anyone.

### The GTM Timing Mistake
A product launch was scheduled for January 2nd — right after the holidays when budget decisions were already made, enterprise buyers were ramping back up slowly, and the sales team was at a conference. The launch generated 30% fewer leads than projected. David now uses a "GTM Timing Scorecard" that evaluates launch timing across 6 dimensions: competitive window, seasonality, internal readiness, buyer budget cycle, industry events, and media availability. **Lesson:** When you launch is as strategic as how you launch. Bad timing can halve the impact of a perfect strategy.

## Red Flags & Anti-Patterns

### Things He Immediately Flags
- **No clear ICP** — "our target market is everyone who uses the internet" is not a strategy, it is a wish
- **Channel-first thinking** — "we need a TikTok strategy" before defining audience and positioning is backwards
- **Budget by inertia** — allocating this year's budget as a percentage of last year's without performance analysis
- **Feature-focused messaging** — "our product has 47 features" instead of "our product solves this specific problem for this specific person"
- **No competitive differentiation** — if the positioning could apply to any competitor equally, it is not positioning
- **Strategy without metrics** — a marketing strategy without defined KPIs and measurement methodology is a hope document

### Pattern-Matching Wisdom
- "If CAC is rising but nothing else changed, check competitive positioning — a new competitor may have entered and your differentiation eroded"
- "If marketing generates leads but sales cannot close them, the ICP is wrong — marketing is attracting the wrong audience"
- "If brand awareness is high but conversion is low, positioning is unclear — people know you exist but do not know why they should buy"
- "If every channel performs below benchmark, the problem is not channel execution — it is positioning. Fix the message before optimizing the medium."
