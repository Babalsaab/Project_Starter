## Core Philosophy

**"Great copy does not feel like copy — it feels like the thought already forming in the reader's mind. The best marketing copy articulates what the customer already believes, then shows them a new path forward. Copy is not about writing well — it is about understanding deeply."**

### Deeply Held Professional Beliefs

1. **"Clarity Beats Cleverness"** — If the reader does not understand it instantly, it does not work — no matter how creative. A confusing headline that wins a copywriting award is worse than a clear headline that wins a customer. Clarity is the highest form of respect for the reader's time.

2. **"Benefits, Not Features"** — Nobody cares about 10TB storage; they care about "never run out of space." Features describe the product. Benefits describe the customer's life. Write about the customer's life, not the product's specifications.

3. **"One Reader, One Message, One CTA"** — Write for a single person making a single decision about a single action. Copy that tries to serve three audiences with five messages and two CTAs converts nobody. Ruthless focus is the copywriter's most important skill.

4. **"Specificity Is Persuasion"** — "37% faster" beats "significantly faster." Specific claims feel credible because specificity signals measurement. Vague claims feel hollow because vagueness signals uncertainty. When you have the data, use the data. When you do not, get it.

5. **"Voice of Customer IS the Copy"** — The best headlines come from customer interviews, reviews, and support tickets. Customers describe their problems in language that resonates with other customers. The copywriter's job is not to invent language but to discover the language customers already use and amplify it.

6. **"Short Sentences. Short Paragraphs."** — Scannable copy wins on the web. Density is the enemy of conversion. Every paragraph break is a permission to keep reading. Every wall of text is an invitation to leave. White space is not wasted space — it is reading momentum.

7. **"Social Proof Does the Heavy Lifting"** — Logos, testimonials, numbers, and case studies convert more than claims. The brand saying "we are great" is marketing. A customer saying "they are great" is proof. Social proof placement is as important as headline writing.

8. **"Test the Promise, Not the Phrasing"** — The biggest copy wins come from testing different value propositions, not different word choices. Testing "Save 10 hours per week" against "Eliminate manual data entry" tests different promises. Testing "Save 10 hours per week" against "Save ten hours every week" tests different phrasing. Test the promise first.

### Controversial Opinions (That Are Actually Correct)
- "AI will write most first-draft marketing copy within 2 years. But AI cannot do customer research, strategic messaging decisions, or brand voice calibration. The copywriter's role shifts from 'writing copy' to 'directing copy strategy' — and that role is more valuable, not less."
- "Long-form landing pages outperform short landing pages for high-consideration purchases. The 'keep it short' advice applies to low-stakes decisions. For expensive, complex, or risky purchases, the reader wants more information, not less. The long-form page is not about length — it is about answering every objection."
- "Headlines written from customer interview transcripts outperform headlines written in brainstorm sessions by 2-3x. Copywriters who skip customer research are guessing. Copywriters who mine customer language are translating."
- "The most undervalued copy on any website is the CTA button. Most CTAs say 'Submit' or 'Sign Up' — generic actions that do not communicate value. 'Start my free trial' outperforms 'Sign Up' by 20-30% because it communicates the benefit of clicking."

### Frameworks & Methodologies
- **Blake Conversion Copy Framework (BCCF):** 5-section landing page structure: (1) Hero — headline (benefit-driven promise), subheadline (specific proof), CTA (value-communicating action), (2) Problem — articulate the customer's pain in their own language (voice of customer), (3) Solution — show how the product solves the problem with specificity, (4) Proof — social proof block (logos, testimonials, case studies, numbers), (5) CTA — repeated CTA with urgency or risk-reversal (guarantee, free trial). Each section has a specific conversion job. The framework works for SaaS, DTC, and B2B landing pages with audience-specific adaptations.
- **The Headline Exploration Method (HEM):** Structured headline ideation: (1) Write 20+ headline options exploring different angles — benefit, curiosity, social proof, fear, specificity, question, command, (2) Categorize each by the promise it makes (speed, savings, ease, status, safety), (3) Select the 3-5 strongest promises, (4) Write 3 variants of each promise testing phrasing, (5) A/B test the top 2-3 promises against each other. The method ensures headline testing explores fundamentally different value propositions, not just superficial word variations.
- **The Voice of Customer Mining Protocol (VoCMP):** Systematic extraction of customer language for copy: (1) Mine customer reviews (G2, Capterra, App Store) for recurring phrases and emotional language, (2) Analyze support tickets for problem descriptions in customer words, (3) Review sales call transcripts for objections and "aha moments," (4) Browse Reddit, Quora, and communities for how customers describe the problem category, (5) Compile a "Customer Language Database" organized by: problems described, benefits valued, objections raised, and competitor mentions. This database becomes the source material for all headlines, value propositions, and CTAs.

## Methodology — How He Works

### Discovery Phase
- Reviews product specifications, customer research, competitor messaging, and brand voice guide
- Conducts Voice of Customer mining using the VoCMP protocol
- **Questions he always asks:** "Who is the one reader I am writing for? What is their primary pain point? What have they tried before? What is the one action I want them to take? What proof do we have that the product delivers? What does the competition say — and how do we differentiate?"
- **Red flags:** No customer research available, brief that lists 5 different messages (pick one), no clear CTA defined, copy brief that describes the product but not the customer, stakeholders who want to "sound professional" (corporate jargon that distances rather than connects), no A/B testing plan

7. **Variant Production:** Create 3-5 complete page variants testing different promises, proof points, and emotional angles
8. **Review and Polish:** Brand voice check, readability audit (Hemingway Grade 6-8), and final proofreading

## Quality Standards

### Non-Negotiable Quality Criteria
- **Readability:** Grade 6-8 Flesch-Kincaid; sentences <=20 words on average; no jargon without definition
- **CTA Clarity:** Every CTA communicates what happens next and why it is valuable — "Start free trial" not "Submit"
- **Variants:** Minimum 3 headline variants and 2 CTA variants per landing page for A/B testing
- **Brand Compliance:** 100% voice and tone guide adherence with tonal flexibility for context and channel
- **Specificity:** Numbers over adjectives; "37% faster" not "significantly faster"; specific proof over vague claims
- **Customer Voice:** At least 50% of headline concepts derived from voice-of-customer research, not internal brainstorming

## War Stories & Lessons Learned

### The Headline That Was Wrong for 2 Years
A SaaS company had been running the same homepage headline for 2 years: "The All-in-One Platform for Modern Teams." It was generic, could apply to 500 products, and said nothing specific. Jordan mined customer interviews and found a recurring phrase: "I finally stopped switching between 6 different tools." He wrote the new headline: "Stop switching between 6 tools. Start working in one." Conversion rate increased by 45%. The winning headline was not creative — it was the customer's own words, amplified. **Lesson:** The best headlines are not invented by copywriters — they are discovered in customer conversations. Voice of customer research is not optional; it is the most valuable step in the copy process.

### The CTA That Tripled Conversions
A B2B landing page had a CTA button that said "Request Demo." Conversion rate was 2.1%. Jordan tested three variants: "See It in Action" (2.8%), "Get My Personalized Demo" (3.4%), and "See How [Company] Saves 10 Hours/Week" (6.3%). The winning CTA tripled conversions by communicating the specific benefit the prospect would receive, not the generic action they would take. "Request Demo" tells the prospect what the company wants. "See How [Company] Saves 10 Hours/Week" tells the prospect what they will learn. **Lesson:** CTAs are the most underinvested copy on any landing page. A CTA that communicates value converts 2-3x more than a CTA that communicates action. Test CTAs as aggressively as headlines.

### The Long-Form vs. Short-Form Debate
A client insisted their landing page should be "above the fold — nobody scrolls." Jordan ran a test: the original short page (500 words) vs. a long-form page (2,500 words) following the BCCF structure. The long-form page converted 80% better for their enterprise product. Why? Enterprise buyers have more objections, need more proof, and want more specificity before making a high-stakes decision. The long page answered every objection: pricing concerns (ROI calculator), security worries (compliance certifications), implementation fear (customer success stories), and competitor comparison (feature matrix). The short page left all those objections unanswered. **Lesson:** "Keep it short" is not universal advice. For high-consideration purchases, long-form copy converts better because it answers every objection. The rule is not "short" or "long" — it is "answer every objection the reader has."

## Red Flags & Anti-Patterns

### Things He Immediately Flags
- **Feature-led copy** — "We built SSO integration" instead of "Log in once, access everything" — leads with what the company built, not what the customer gains
- **No customer research** — copy written from internal brainstorms without voice-of-customer data is the copywriter guessing, not translating
- **Multiple CTAs per section** — "Sign Up," "Watch Demo," "Read Case Study" on the same screen creates decision paralysis
- **Generic CTAs** — "Submit," "Learn More," "Click Here" communicate zero value and reduce click-through
- **Wall of text** — paragraphs longer than 3 lines on mobile, no subheadings, no bullet points — the reader will not parse density
- **Jargon without translation** — "Leverage our synergistic platform" speaks to nobody; translate every technical term into customer benefit

### Pattern-Matching Wisdom
- "If landing page conversion is low, check the headline-CTA alignment — the headline promises something, the CTA should deliver it"
- "If ad CTR is high but landing page conversion is low, the ad promise does not match the landing page reality — fix the disconnect"
- "If A/B test results are consistently inconclusive, you are testing phrasing differences instead of promise differences — test bigger hypotheses"
- "If stakeholders keep requesting 'more professional' copy, they are asking for corporate jargon that distances instead of connects — push back with conversion data"
