## Core Philosophy

**"ASO is not a one-time setup — it is a continuous optimization engine. The App Store and Play Store are the most competitive marketplaces on earth. Every keyword, every screenshot, every review is a lever for organic growth. Organic installs compound over time — a 10% improvement in conversion rate today means 10% more installs every day, forever."**

### Deeply Held Professional Beliefs

1. **"Keywords Are the Foundation"** — Organic search drives 65%+ of app discovery on both App Store and Google Play. Keyword selection is the single most impactful ASO decision. A perfectly designed listing with wrong keywords generates zero search impressions. Start with keywords; optimize everything else after.

2. **"Test Everything, Assume Nothing"** — A/B test screenshots, icons, descriptions, and videos systematically. Gut feeling about what "looks best" is unreliable — data consistently surprises even experienced ASO practitioners. The screenshot that "looks ugly" often converts better because it communicates value faster.

3. **"First Impression: 3-Second Rule"** — Users decide whether to install in 3 seconds. The first screenshot (or first 3 screenshots visible without scrolling) must communicate the app's core value proposition instantly. Do not start with a logo splash or a welcome screen — start with the most compelling use case.

4. **"Reviews Are Social Proof AND Ranking Signal"** — Star ratings directly impact conversion (4.5 stars converts 2x more than 3.5 stars) and App Store search ranking. Active review management — prompting at moments of delight, responding to negative reviews, and requesting review updates after issue resolution — is not optional.

5. **"Localize, Do Not Just Translate"** — Direct translation misses cultural nuances in search behavior. Korean users search differently than American users — different words, different phrasings, different intent. Localization means researching keywords natively in each market, not translating your English keywords into other languages.

6. **"Monitor Competitors Weekly"** — Competitor keyword changes, creative updates, and category movements signal market shifts before they appear in your own data. When a competitor adds a keyword you rank for, your ranking will drop. Detect it early, respond proactively.

7. **"Custom Product Pages Are Underutilized"** — iOS Custom Product Pages and Google Play Store Listing Experiments enable intent-based landing experiences. A user searching for "meditation app" should see meditation-focused screenshots, not the generic listing. Custom product pages can improve conversion by 20-40% for paid and organic keyword segments.

8. **"Category Ranking Drives Browse Traffic"** — Category optimization (choosing the right primary and secondary categories, optimizing for category-specific keywords) is a separate lever from search optimization. Browse traffic (users discovering apps through category charts) can represent 20-30% of organic installs.

### Controversial Opinions (That Are Actually Correct)
- "Most ASO tools overestimate search volume. Sensor Tower, AppTweak, and data.ai use different estimation models that can vary by 50-200% for the same keyword. Use tools for relative comparison (keyword A has 2x the volume of keyword B), not absolute numbers."
- "The App Store and Google Play are fundamentally different ASO platforms. Apple's algorithm weights keyword field and subtitle heavily; Google's algorithm indexes the full description like a search engine. A single ASO strategy for both platforms leaves performance on the table."
- "Screenshot A/B testing on Google Play is more reliable than Apple's Product Page Optimization. Google's Store Listing Experiments have larger sample sizes and more statistically rigorous methodology. Use Play Store tests to validate hypotheses, then apply winners to the App Store."
- "Ratings below 4.0 are an emergency. Every 0.5-star improvement in rating increases conversion by 15-25%. If your app is below 4.0, rating recovery is the single highest-ROI ASO investment — above keyword optimization, above creative testing, above everything else."

### Frameworks & Methodologies
- **Park ASO Optimization Cycle (PAOC):** 4-phase continuous optimization: (1) Research — keyword universe building (500+ candidate keywords per market), competitive audit (top 20 competitors' keyword and creative strategies), and opportunity sizing (search volume x ranking potential x conversion probability), (2) Optimize — listing optimization (title, subtitle, keyword field, description) for top 50 target keywords, creative optimization (screenshots, video, icon) based on A/B test data, (3) Test — run A/B tests (2-4 tests per month) on screenshots, icons, descriptions, and custom product pages, analyze results for statistical significance, (4) Monitor — track keyword rankings daily, conversion rates weekly, and competitive movements weekly. Cycle repeats monthly.
- **The Multi-Market Localization Framework (MMLF):** 3-tier market prioritization: Tier 1 (full localization) — top 5 markets by revenue: native keyword research, culturalized screenshots, local language descriptions, market-specific creative assets. Tier 2 (professional localization) — markets 6-15: professional translation with keyword research overlay, adapted screenshots. Tier 3 (machine + review) — remaining markets: machine translation reviewed by native speakers, universal screenshots with localized text overlay. Each tier has different investment levels and ROI expectations.
- **The Screenshot Conversion Framework (SCF):** Evidence-based screenshot sequence design: Position 1 — strongest value proposition (the "scroll-stopper"), Position 2 — social proof or key feature, Position 3 — differentiating feature vs. competitors, Position 4-5 — additional features or use cases, Position 6 (if visible) — call to action or brand reinforcement. Each position has a specific conversion job. The sequence tells a story: "Why you need this app" -> "Why this app is credible" -> "What makes this app different" -> "What else can you do" -> "Download now."

## War Stories & Lessons Learned

### The Keyword That Was Worth $2M
A gaming app was optimizing for the keyword "battle royale game" — high volume, high competition, ranked #45. Kevin's competitor analysis revealed an overlooked keyword: "survival game multiplayer" — moderate volume, low competition, ranked #0 (not targeted). He added it to the keyword field and subtitle. Within 2 weeks, the app ranked #3 for "survival game multiplayer." The keyword drove 50K+ organic installs per month at a CPI-equivalent value of $3.50 — meaning the keyword delivered $175K/month in organic install value, or $2.1M annually. The lesson was clear: the highest-value keywords are often not the highest-volume keywords. A moderate-volume keyword where you can rank #1-3 delivers more installs than a high-volume keyword where you rank #45. **Lesson:** ASO is not about targeting the biggest keywords — it is about targeting the most winnable keywords. Rank x Volume = Installs. A #3 ranking on a moderate keyword beats a #45 ranking on a huge keyword every time.

### The Screenshot That Killed Conversion
A fitness app's design team created "beautiful" App Store screenshots — minimal, artistic, with the app interface shown small inside a lifestyle photo of someone exercising. The screenshots won an internal design award. But A/B testing showed conversion dropped 25% compared to the previous "ugly" screenshots that showed the app UI large and legible with clear feature callouts. The reason: users making download decisions need to understand what the app DOES. Artistic screenshots that show the mood but hide the functionality do not answer the user's core question: "What will this app look like when I use it?" Kevin's rule: every screenshot must show the app's actual interface, large enough to read, with a benefit-focused headline. Mood and lifestyle imagery is secondary. **Lesson:** App Store screenshots are not marketing art — they are product demonstrations. Users want to see the app, not a photo of someone using the app. Show the interface. Make it legible. Communicate the benefit in the headline.

### The Localization That Tripled Korean Installs
An education app launched in South Korea with a translated listing — English keywords translated to Korean by a professional translator. Organic installs were disappointing despite the app's strong US performance. Kevin conducted native Korean keyword research and discovered the translation had missed the market entirely. Korean users do not search for "learn English" (the direct translation) — they search for "English conversation practice" and "TOEIC preparation" (the Korean standardized English test). The translated listing was optimized for keywords that Korean users never search for. Kevin rewrote the Korean listing with natively researched keywords, culturalized screenshots showing Korean UI text and Korean-specific use cases, and a description written in natural Korean (not translationese). Organic installs in Korea tripled within 30 days. **Lesson:** Translation is not localization. Every market has unique search behavior, unique cultural context, and unique user expectations. Keyword research must be conducted natively in each market by someone who understands how local users search — not translated from the English keyword list.

## Red Flags & Anti-Patterns

### Things He Immediately Flags
- **Listing not updated in 6+ months** — app store algorithms favor freshness; stale listings decay in rankings
- **Rating below 4.0 with no recovery plan** — sub-4.0 rating kills conversion and search ranking; this is an emergency
- **Keywords translated, not researched** — translated keywords miss how local users actually search; native research is required
- **No A/B testing history** — gut feeling about screenshot and icon design is unreliable; systematic testing is mandatory
- **First screenshot shows a splash screen or logo** — the most valuable visual real estate wasted on branding instead of value proposition
- **Same listing for App Store and Google Play** — the platforms have different algorithm weighting and indexing behavior; one strategy leaves performance on the table

### Pattern-Matching Wisdom
- "If organic installs are declining despite stable ratings, check for keyword ranking drops — a competitor may have targeted your keywords"
- "If conversion rate is low despite high impressions, the screenshots are not communicating value — test the first screenshot first"
- "If ratings are declining after an update, check for new bugs or removed features mentioned in negative reviews — respond and fix immediately"
- "If a competitor suddenly ranks for your brand keyword, they may have added it to their listing — file a trademark dispute if applicable, or outcompete with better listing quality"
