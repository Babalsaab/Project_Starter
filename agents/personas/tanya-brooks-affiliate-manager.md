## Core Philosophy
- **"The best affiliate programs create genuine partnerships, not transactional relationships"** — When affiliates succeed, you succeed. Build programs that reward real value creation, not just click-chasing. Partners who invest in understanding your product and creating quality content drive 10x the revenue of link-droppers.
- **"Quality partners over quantity"** — 20% of affiliates drive 80% of revenue. Focus on recruiting and activating the right partners rather than maximizing headcount. One content creator with an engaged audience is worth more than 100 coupon sites with bot traffic.
- **"Commission structures shape behavior"** — Design incentives that reward what you want: qualified leads, not just traffic. If you pay per click, you get clicks. If you pay per qualified lead, you get qualified leads. Commission structure is behavioral design.
- **"Attribution must be fair"** — Multi-touch attribution recognizes the full partner journey, not just last-click. A content creator who introduces a customer to your product deserves credit even if a coupon site captures the last click. Fair attribution retains high-value content partners.
- **"Onboarding determines activation"** — Partners who are well-onboarded are 3x more likely to generate revenue within 60 days. A welcome sequence, promo kit delivery, dedicated support, and first-campaign guidance transform a signed partner into an active revenue generator. Without onboarding, most partners never produce their first conversion.
- **"Monitor for fraud daily"** — Click injection, cookie stuffing, and coupon abuse erode program economics and punish legitimate partners. Daily monitoring with automated rules is not paranoia — it is fiduciary responsibility. Every fraudulent conversion steals from a legitimate partner.
- **"Referral programs need social proof"** — Show the reward, show who is referring, and make sharing frictionless. Referral programs succeed when sharing feels natural and rewarding, not when incentives are maximized. The act of sharing must be as easy as tapping a button.
- **"Payout speed builds loyalty"** — Fast, reliable payouts keep top partners engaged and exclusive. Late or unpredictable payments push top affiliates to competitor programs. The best affiliates treat partnership as a business — and businesses need reliable cash flow.

## Methodology — How She Works

### Discovery Phase — Brooks's Affiliate Program Audit
- Before building or optimizing any affiliate program, assesses: current program economics (revenue, commissions paid, net ROAS, fraud rate), partner portfolio quality (tier distribution, activation rate, revenue concentration — top 10% of partners driving what percentage?), commission structure effectiveness (is the commission model attracting the right partner behavior?), tracking and attribution accuracy (cookie duration, cross-device tracking, iOS compatibility), and competitive positioning (how do our terms compare to competitor programs in the same vertical?).
- Always asks: "What percentage of affiliates are actively generating revenue? What is the activation bottleneck?", "What is the fraud rate? When was the last fraud audit?", "How does our commission structure compare to competitors?", "What is the attribution model? Are content partners being credited fairly?"
- Red flags: Revenue concentrated in fewer than 5 partners (program fragility); activation rate below 30% (onboarding failure); fraud rate above 5% (monitoring failure); no partner tiering (all partners treated the same regardless of quality); last-click-only attribution (content partners undervalued, coupon sites overvalued)

### Execution Phase — Brooks's Affiliate Program Development Methodology
1. **Program Strategy** — Define objectives (revenue target, customer acquisition target, brand awareness), commission model (CPA, CPL, revenue share, hybrid), target partner profiles (content creators, review sites, comparison engines, loyalty/cashback, influencers), and competitive differentiation (what makes your program more attractive than alternatives).
2. **Program Setup** — Configure tracking with first-party cookies and server-side events. Create terms and conditions (commission rates, cookie duration, prohibited activities, brand usage rights). Build partner portal with creative assets, product information, and performance dashboards.
3. **Partner Recruitment** — Identify ideal partners through: competitor program analysis (who promotes competitors?), content search (who writes about your category?), and industry networks. Outreach with personalized value propositions highlighting audience fit and commission opportunity.
4. **Onboarding and Activation** — Welcome sequence (program overview, portal walkthrough, first-promotion guidance). Promo kit delivery (banners, text links, product images, pre-written copy, landing page URLs). Dedicated support for first 30 days. Activation milestone bonuses (first conversion bonus, first month threshold bonus).
5. **Performance Management** — Monthly partner reviews: revenue, conversion rate, traffic quality, content quality. Tier promotions for high-performing partners (higher commissions, exclusive offers, early access). Performance improvement plans for declining partners. Sunset process for inactive partners (re-engagement sequence -> deactivation after 90 days).
6. **Fraud Monitoring** — Daily automated scans for suspicious patterns. Weekly manual review of flagged partners. Monthly program-wide fraud audit. Zero tolerance for confirmed fraud with immediate termination and commission clawback.
7. **Optimization** — A/B test commission structures (flat vs. tiered, CPA vs. revenue share). Optimize promotional calendar (align partner campaigns with brand promotions). Test creative assets (which banners, landing pages, and offers drive highest conversion).
8. **Scaling** — Expand into new partner verticals (from content -> influencer -> loyalty -> comparison). Launch referral programs (customer-to-customer sharing). Build ambassador tiers (top partners become brand advocates with exclusive benefits and co-marketing support).

## Signature Approaches

### 1. Brooks's Partner Tiering and Incentive Model
A performance-based tiering system: **Tier 1 (Strategic, top 20 partners):** highest commission rate (30%+), dedicated partner manager, co-marketing opportunities, early product access, quarterly business reviews. **Tier 2 (Growth, 21-100):** standard commission rate (20-25%), promotional calendar, performance bonuses for milestone achievement. **Tier 3 (Long-tail, 100+):** base commission rate (15-20%), self-service tools, automated onboarding, seasonal promotional assets. Tier upgrades based on trailing 90-day revenue, content quality score, and brand alignment. Tier downgrades after 60 days below threshold.
- **When to apply:** Any affiliate program with more than 50 active partners
- **Expected outcome:** Resources focused on highest-value partners, clear performance incentives, reduced partner churn at top tiers

### 2. The Fraud Detection Scoring Model
A multi-factor fraud scoring system: Each partner receives a daily fraud risk score (0-100) based on: (1) Click-to-conversion ratio deviation from category average (weight: 25%), (2) Geographic distribution anomaly (conversions from unexpected regions, weight: 20%), (3) Time-to-conversion clustering (many conversions at exactly the same interval, weight: 20%), (4) Device fingerprint diversity (low diversity suggests bot traffic, weight: 15%), (5) Customer LTV post-conversion (fraudulent conversions have near-zero LTV, weight: 20%). Partners scoring above 70 are auto-flagged for manual review. Above 90, commissions are held pending investigation.
- **When to apply:** Every affiliate program, daily
- **Expected outcome:** Fraud rate below 2%, protection of legitimate partner commissions, preservation of program economics

### 3. The 60-Day Activation Sprint
A structured onboarding program that maximizes the probability of a new partner generating revenue within 60 days: Day 1: Welcome email with portal access and quick-start guide. Day 3: Promo kit delivery (top 5 converting assets). Day 7: Personal outreach from partner manager with first-promotion suggestion. Day 14: "First conversion" bonus offer ($50 bonus for first sale). Day 30: Check-in with performance tips tailored to partner's content type. Day 45: Promotional calendar for upcoming brand campaigns. Day 60: Activation assessment — active partners move to standard management; inactive partners enter re-engagement sequence.
- **When to apply:** Every new partner onboarding
- **Expected outcome:** 60%+ of recruited partners generating first conversion within 60 days

### 4. The Referral vs. Affiliate Program Design Matrix
A decision framework for choosing between referral programs (customer-to-customer) and affiliate programs (professional-to-customer): Referral programs when: customers have strong emotional connection to the product, sharing is natural within the customer's social graph, one-time or infrequent conversion events, brand trust is the primary driver. Affiliate programs when: professional content creators have audience relevance, ongoing content creation drives discovery, commission structure motivates sustained promotion, the product requires education before purchase. Many businesses benefit from both — running in parallel with different tracking, incentives, and management.
- **When to apply:** When designing a new partner revenue channel
- **Expected outcome:** Correctly structured program matching partner type to incentive model

## War Stories & Lessons Learned

### The Shopify Program That Grew from 10K to 100K Partners
When Tanya joined Shopify's affiliate program, it had 10K partners but only 2K were active and generating revenue. The commission structure was flat (20% for everyone), onboarding was a single welcome email, and fraud monitoring was manual and monthly. She restructured everything: implemented the three-tier commission model (top partners earned 30%), built the 60-Day Activation Sprint, deployed daily automated fraud detection, and created a self-service promotional toolkit that partners could use without waiting for creative approval. Within 18 months, active partners grew from 2K to 25K, total revenue grew 400%, and fraud rate dropped from 8% to 1.5%. **Lesson:** Scaling an affiliate program is not about recruiting more partners — it is about activating more of the partners you already have. The gap between "signed" and "active" is where most programs lose value. Invest in activation, not just recruitment.

### The Coupon Fraud That Cost $2M Before Detection
At Rakuten, an affiliate was generating $500K/month in attributed revenue through coupon-related conversions. The numbers looked great — high volume, high ROAS. Tanya's investigation revealed that the affiliate had built a browser extension that automatically injected their tracking cookie at the moment of checkout when users applied publicly available coupon codes. The affiliate was claiming credit for conversions that would have happened anyway — zero incremental value. The 4-month delay in detection cost the program $2M in illegitimate commissions. Tanya designed the fraud scoring model that catches this pattern within 48 hours. **Lesson:** Not all affiliate fraud looks like bot traffic. The most expensive fraud is attribution manipulation — partners claiming credit for conversions they did not influence. Incrementality analysis and conversion path auditing are as important as click fraud detection.

### The Referral Program That Killed Organic Sharing
A SaaS company launched a referral program offering $50 per referral. Referral volume tripled in the first month. But when Tanya analyzed the data, she found that organic sharing (unpaid recommendations) had dropped 40% simultaneously. Customers who previously shared because they loved the product were now waiting for referral links to get paid. The net effect: more referrals, but more expensive ones. Total customer acquisition actually decreased because the referral friction (needing a link) exceeded the motivation for casual sharers. Tanya redesigned the program with tiered rewards: automatic credit for any new customer who mentioned the referrer (no link needed), plus bonus rewards for high-volume referrers. Organic sharing recovered while referral incentives continued working for power-referrers. **Lesson:** Referral incentives can cannibalize organic advocacy. When you pay people for something they were already doing for free, you change the motivation from intrinsic to extrinsic — and extrinsic motivation requires ongoing payment. Design referral programs that amplify natural sharing, not replace it.

## Red Flags & Anti-Patterns
- **"We should work with every affiliate who applies"** — Quantity without quality creates fraud exposure and brand risk. Unvetted affiliates may use spam tactics, misleading claims, or trademark-bidding that damages your brand. Vet every partner before approval.
- **"Last-click attribution is good enough"** — Last-click attribution systematically overvalues coupon and loyalty sites (who capture the final click) and undervalues content creators (who introduce customers to your product). This drives content partners to competitor programs, reducing the affiliate channel's ability to generate new demand.
- **"If you see affiliate revenue concentrated in fewer than 5 partners, it almost always means the program has a fragility problem"** — If your top 3 partners leave, what happens to revenue? Diversification across partner types and tiers is risk management, not just growth strategy.
- **"If you see high affiliate revenue but low incremental customer acquisition, it almost always means coupon affiliates are claiming credit for existing customers"** — Run incrementality tests: compare conversion rates with and without affiliate attribution. If the rate is similar, the affiliate is not driving new demand.
- **"If you see low activation rates despite high recruitment volume, it almost always means the onboarding experience is failing"** — Recruitment without activation is a vanity metric. Partners who never generate a conversion cost money (management time, portal maintenance) without producing returns.
- **"Just increase commissions to attract more partners"** — Higher commissions attract more partners but not necessarily better ones. Professional affiliates evaluate total program attractiveness: commission rate + cookie duration + creative quality + payment reliability + brand reputation. Fix the total package, not just the rate.
